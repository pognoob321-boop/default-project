## 1. Choose Your Topic: Two Approaches

### 1.1 Top-Down Approach
**Video**: Choose title/focus first, then research. Quicker, intuitive. Risk: confirmation bias.

### 1.2 Bottom-Up Approach
**Video**: Start with research (foraging). Topic emerges. More time-consuming but better when direction unclear. Modern researchers work this way.

### 1.3 Bridging the Two
**Video**: Start bottom-up, switch to top-down to fill gaps.
**[User]**: " Go from bottom-up to top-down. Lock in topic research hard. Scholars notice interesting things and research them deeply.\

## 2. Define Depth and Breadth

**Video**: Cannot research vast fields. Refine for realistic progress. Depth-breadth matrix:

| Dimension | Low | High |
|-----------|-----|------|
| **Depth** | High-school level | PhD+, ultra-technical |
| **Breadth** | Super general | Ultra-niche (e.g., " intersectional feminism in Warhammer 40K hive cities\) |

Generally cannot maximize both. Modern research leans deep+narrow. Hobbyists can compromise. Not locked in.

**[User]**: Depth = effort level. Breadth = niche vs general.

## 3. Gather Literature

### 3.1 Start Small, Follow Citations
**Video**: Do not catalog upfront. Start 1-2 sources; follow citations.

### 3.2 Shallow Media as Entry Points
**Video**: Use videos, articles, Wikipedia to build mental map, identify key terms. No shame. Example: moral anti-realism via interview -> wiki -> ready for papers.
**[User]**: Entry point = digestible media for concept map. Stay relevant. Find keywords.

### 3.3 Deeper Sources
**Video**: Google Scholar, JSTOR, ScienceDirect, ResearchGate, Semantic Scholar, **Consensus**. Paywall workarounds: filetype:pdf, institutional access, Anna Archive, Sci-Hub, email author.

### 3.4 Books
**Video**: Find authoritative big dogs. Inspect before committing. Do not force-read entire books. Papers: abstract, conclusion, discussion first.
**[User]**: Multiple sources (6+), different POVs. Translations are filters. Do not lose references - use Zotero, Research Rabbit, Consensus.

### 3.5 Translations
**Video**: Not literal; change tone, inject interpreter views. Example: Odyssey translations.

## 5. Workflow: The 5-Level Funnel

**Video**: Scattered ideas -> linear order via funnel. Bottom = commonplace book (Obsidian, notes, mind maps).

| Level | Name | Purpose |
|-------|------|---------|
| 1 | **Inspection** | Relevant at glance (blurb, abstract) |
| 2 | **To Read** | Worth deeper look |
| 3 | **Insights** | Most relevant/interesting ideas |
| 4 | **Order** | Story notes tell together, synthesis |
| 5 | *(Implicit)* | **Commonplace book** - ideas combine |

**Video**: Most new knowledge is synthesis. Promote synthesis.
**[User]**: Tips: 1) Rephrase in own words. 2) Identify problems/limits. 3) Connect concepts. 4) Create index (contents page).

## 6. Critical Thinking

### 6.1 The CRAAP Test

| Letter | Criterion | Questions |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| **C** | **Current** | Recent? Valid? Obsolete? |
| **R** | **Relevance** | Contributes to YOUR conversation? Same topic != same angle. |
| **A** | **Authority** | Respected journal? Peer review? Expert author? Cited? |
| **A** | **Accuracy** | Logical arguments? Evidence? Self-aware? Limits acknowledged? |
| **P** | **Purpose** | Author goal? Funding? Context? |

**[User]**: Relevance = different POVs aligning with your view. My interpretation.

### 6.2 Critical Thinking Tips

1. **Read multiple sources** - one book = one biased person. Cross-reference, consensus, spot conflicts.
2. **Hesitate to make judgments** - doubt yourself. Want to be proven wrong. Accept you are not open-minded.
3. **Seek challenges** - confirmation bias = jerking yourself off. Read disagreeable material. Changing mind = strength.
4. **Break down paragraphs** - points, arguments, qualifiers. Check: evidence backs point? Assumptions? Limits?
   **[User]**: Breaks paragraph into claims/evidence/logic. My interpretation of image.
5. **Understand your limits** - papers written for researchers (in the club), not laymen (NPC plebs). Brute-force wastes time. Use shallow media (aware they are filters).

## 7. Output: Give Yourself a Goal

**Video**: Study meaningless without purpose. University has external motivators. Self-directed? Intrinsic fails when tough. **Output = accountability**. Produce something: video, article, book, essay. Forces polish/structure for others. Every successful creator just talked about passion publicly.
**[User]**: Output = future goal prevents drift. Write for future. My interpretation.
