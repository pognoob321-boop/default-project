# Acne Treatment

## Part 1: Eliminate Active Acne First

### Lifestyle
- Eat a diet that doesn't trigger your acne
- Limit high glycemic foods if they worsen breakouts
- Some people improve by reducing dairy
- Don't pick or squeeze pimples
- Change pillowcases regularly
- Wash your face after sweating
- Wear sunscreen daily

### Topical Treatments
- Benzoyl peroxide
- Salicylic acid
- Adapalene
- Tretinoin
- Azelaic acid
- Gentle cleanser
- Non-comedogenic moisturizer

### Prescription Options
- Oral antibiotics (short term)
- Hormonal treatments (when appropriate)
- Isotretinoin (Accutane) for severe or persistent acne
- See a dermatologist if OTC products fail

## Part 1b: Closed Comedones (whiteheads)

> Verified 2026-10-17 against AAD 2024 acne guideline (Reynolds et al., JAAD,
> doi 10.1016/j.jaad.2023.09.030) and the Canadian clinical practice guideline
> (Asai et al., PMC4732962). **Salicylic acid is the internet's default answer
> and it is the wrong one for closed comedones specifically** — see below.

### What they are

A **microcomedone**: keratin and sebum plug the follicle and the pore opening
closes over. Nothing oxidizes, so it stays skin-colored — unlike an open
comedone (blackhead) where the exposed plug darkens. Per AAD 2024, the
microcomedone is the precursor lesion of *every* form of acne, which is why the
retinoid literature keeps naming it as the treatment target.

The four pathogenic factors (AAD 2024): follicular hyperkeratinization,
*C. acnes* colonization, sebum production, inflammation. Closed comedones are
primarily the **hyperkeratinization** arm, not the bacterial arm.

### Treatment hierarchy

| Agent | AAD 2024 rec | Certainty | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Topical retinoid** (adapalene, tretinoin, tazarotene, trifarotene) | **Strong** | Moderate | Comedolytic + anti-inflammatory. The cornerstone. |
| **Benzoyl peroxide 2.5–5%** | **Strong** | Moderate | Mildly comedolytic, bactericidal, onset in days. |
| **Fixed adapalene + BP** (Epiduo) | **Strong** | Moderate | Beats either alone for comedonal acne. |
| Azelaic acid | Conditional | Moderate | Also helps post-inflammatory dyspigmentation. |
| Salicylic acid 0.5–2% | Conditional | **Low** | **No benefit for closed comedones** in the RCT — see below. |
| Clascoterone | Conditional | High | Androgen-receptor blocker, topical. |

**Adapalene and tazarotene are superior to tretinoin for comedonal acne
specifically** (Canadian guideline, level 1 evidence, two grade-A studies each).
Adapalene 0.1% gel is OTC in many markets; tretinoin and tazarotene are
prescription-only.

**Combination therapy** is a good practice statement in AAD 2024: use multiple
agents with different mechanisms rather than one agent at higher strength.

### Salicylic acid: the correction

The popular advice is "use BHA for closed comedones." AAD 2024's only salicylic
acid RCT (0.5%, 12 weeks vs vehicle) found:

- 25% greater reduction in **inflammatory** lesions
- 11% greater reduction in **open** comedones
- **No difference in closed comedones**

Conditional rec, low certainty. Salicylic acid is a reasonable general
exfoliant and is fine for blackheads. It is not the tool for whiteheads, and
the one study that tested it said so explicitly.

### What does not work

- **Squeezing.** A closed comedone has no opening. Pressure drives the plug
  deeper into the follicle and converts it into an inflammatory papule or
  pustule. This is the mechanism of extraction damage, not just "germs."
- **Pore strips.** Adhesive only grabs open comedones with an exposed plug.
- **Physical scrubs and harsh exfoliation.** Irritation, no unclogging.
- **Higher BP concentration.** 2.5–5% is not worse than 10%, and lower is
  better tolerated. Higher concentration = more burning, same comedolytic
  effect.

### The purge — why people quit at week 3

Retinoids accelerate epidermal turnover and resolve *subclinical* microcomedones
by pushing them to the surface. Over weeks 4–6 you see new inflammatory lesions
appear where there were none. **This is the treatment working, not a
reaction.** Quitting at week 3 is the most common failure mode and it produces
the false belief that the product "broke me out."

Full comedonal clearance is **8–12 weeks**. Comedones respond slower than
inflammatory lesions. BP acts faster (days) but does not clear the underlying
microcomedone, which is why it is the *partner*, not the replacement.

### Practical stack

AM: gentle cleanser → azelaic acid (optional) → moisturizer → SPF 30–50.

PM: gentle cleanser → BP 2.5–5% (short contact, 2–5 min, rinse) → adapalene
0.1% or tretinoin → moisturizer.

⚠️ **Compatibility:** tretinoin is inactivated by benzoyl peroxide. Adapalene,
tazarotene, and tretinoin *microsphere* formulations are not — they can be used
together. If your retinoid is plain tretinoin cream, apply BP at a different
time of day or use a wash-off BP.

⚠️ **Start slow.** 2–3 nights per week, build to nightly. Buffer with
moisturizer if needed. Irritation around the cheeks and jawline is the usual
failure, not the comedones themselves.

### When to see a dermatologist

If nothing clears after 12 weeks of consistent retinoid + BP, if the comedones
are accompanied by scarring, or if there is psychosocial distress — per AAD
2024, psychosocial burden counts as severe and makes isotretinoin an option.

## Part 2: Fade Acne Marks (PIH & PIE)

### Daily Routine
- SPF 30-50 every day
- Vitamin C serum
- Niacinamide
- Azelaic acid
- Retinoids
- Gentle chemical exfoliants (AHA/BHA)

### For Red Marks (PIE)
- Time and sun protection
- Azelaic acid
- Hyla acid
- Hydro cort
- Retinoids
- Professional vascular lasers if persistent

### For Dark Marks (PIH)
- Vitamin C
- Retinoids
- Glycolic acid
- Hyla acid
- Chemical exfoliation
- Hydroquinone (short-term under medical supervision)

### Main Goal
- Prevent new acne
- Protect from UV exposure
- Stay consistent for several months

## Part 3: Treat True Acne Scars

### Know Your Scar Type
- Ice pick scars
- Boxcar scars
- Rolling scars
- Hypertrophic/Keloid scars

### Most Effective Treatments
- Microneedling
- RF microneedling
- Fractional laser resurfacing
- TCA CROSS (especially ice pick scars)
- Chemical peels
- Subcision (rolling scars)
- Dermal fillers (selected cases)
- Punch excision for severe ice pick scars

### Important
- Deep scars usually require multiple sessions
- Different scar types need different treatments
- Treat active acne before scar procedures
- Professional evaluation gives the best treatment plan
