## FACIAL ANATOMY: THE SCIENCE

### Key Bones

#### Maxilla (Upper Jaw)
- Supports midface, cheekbones, upper teeth, nose, under-eye
- Grows via intramembranous ossification
- Continues until ~17-21 in males
- Forward growth = better profile

#### Mandible (Lower Jaw)
- Grows via surface remodeling + condylar cartilage
- Growth plates close by early-mid 20s
- Condylar cartilage is secondary cartilage (adaptive to mechanical load)
- **Critical**: Once mineralized (~18-20), cannot grow longitudinally

#### Zygomatic Bones (Cheekbones)
- Frame the midface
- Respond to mechanical stress

#### Ramus (The "Law" Factor)
- Vertical portion of mandible
- **Ramus is Law** = jaw structure determines attractiveness
- Compensates for downgrown maxilla
- Tall ramus = strong bite force, masculine face

### The Ramus Deep Dive (From looksmax.org)

#### What is the Ramus?
- The vertical bone from jaw angle to condyle
- Determines lower face height
- Strongly correlated with bite force

#### "Ramus is Law" Explained
```
When maxilla grows downward → mandible follows
↓
Open bite + high Frankfurt mandibular plane angle
↓
Weak bite + bad aesthetics
↓
Ramus grows to compensate
↓
Lower mandibular plane angle + stronger bite
```

#### Key Insight: Ramus as Compensation
- **Tall ramus** = compensates for downgrown maxilla
- **Forward-grown maxilla** = no need for tall ramus
- Ramus grows during puberty with:
  - High testosterone
  - Good diet
  - Proper habits

#### Masseter Training → Ramus Growth
Evidence from looksmax.org:
- Bite force correlates with ramus length *(VERIFIED — Nickel 2017, PMID 28643919, R²=.49)*
- Farmers (soft diet) = shorter ramus **← WRONG, INVERTED**
- Meat eaters (hard diet) = longer ramus **← WRONG, INVERTED**
- Bruxism patients = 300x more likely to have mandibular angle bone apposition **← NO SOURCE, DELETED**

**Mechanism**: Masseters put mechanical tension on mandible → new bone grows → longer ramus + lower gonial angle

> **Verified 2026-10-14 — this whole block was wrong.**
>
> **1. The diet→ramus relationship is backwards.** The wiki says hard diet
> lengthens the ramus. The research says the opposite:
> - **Katz, Grote & Weaver 2017** (*PNAS*, PMC5576786): historical European
>   **farmers had a *narrower* ramus** than hunter-gatherers on harder diets.
> - **Inoue et al. 2019** (*Sci Rep* 9:1): mice on a **hard diet developed a
>   *shorter* ramus**.
> - The direction of ramus-height vs. bite-force correlation (R²=.49) does hold
>   — that part is fine.
>
> **2. "300x" has no source.** Checked every plausible paper:
> - **Turp 2021** (PMID 34663284): ~50% of mandibular angles apposed in adult
>   bruxism patients, but the control group was **adolescents 12-18**, so no
>   bruxism-vs-nonbruxism odds ratio is computable.
> - **Hayek 2022** (PMID 36199841): 52% apposition, **no control group**.
> - **Popescu 2025** (PMID 40150044): ~3x bone apposition, 15x masseter
>   hypertrophy.
>
> Masseter *muscle* hypertrophy is real. Adding *bone* width by chewing is not
> demonstrated in adults.

### Frankfurt Mandibular Plane Angle (FMA)
- Angle between Frankfort horizontal and mandibular plane
- Lower = better aesthetics
- High FMA = "long face" syndrome

### Mandibular Angle
- Should be close to 90° (square jaw) **← WRONG BY ~35°**
- Obtuse angles = weaker jaw appearance
- Acute angles = stronger, more masculine

> **Verified 2026-10-14: the 90° target is simply wrong.** Normative adult
> gonial angles are far higher:
> - **Riolo et al. 1974** (via Naini FB, *Semin Orthod* 2014): **124 ± 6°** in
>   adult males, 122 ± 4° in females.
> - **Izard 1927**: 120-130°.
> - **Bhullar et al. 2021** (PMC8673450): normodivergent 124.06°, hypodivergent
>   119.94°.
>
> A gonial angle near 90° would be a marked skeletal abnormality, not an
> aesthetic ideal. Note this also contradicts `facial-ratios.md` in this same
> wiki, which lists gonial angle as 112-125° — that range is closer to correct.

## MEWING: THE COMPLETE EVIDENCE

### What It Is
- Named after Dr. John Mew (British orthodontist)
- Proper tongue posture: entire tongue flat against roof of mouth
- Lips sealed, nasal breathing only
- Part of "orthotropics"

### The Claims
1. Reshapes jawline and midface
2. Improves forward growth
3. Enhances cheekbone definition
4. Fixes bite problems

### The Scientific Evidence

#### For (Limited)
- Tongue posture influences childhood facial development
- Oral posture and nasal breathing are real orthodontic principles
- Mouth-breathing children develop longer, narrower faces
- May improve nasal breathing and resting face posture
- Free and risk-free

#### Against (Stronger)
- AAO does not recommend mewing
- No credible evidence for adult bone reshaping
- Tongue pressure: 5-10g vs. 500-1000g needed for skeletal change **← INVENTED NUMBERS**
- Before/after photos = lighting + weight loss
- Adult cranial sutures are fused
- Thesis study: "orthotropics does little... does not influence skeletal pattern differently from normal growth"

> **Verified 2026-10-14 — the tongue-pressure figures are fabricated.** Checked
> against the actual literature:
> - **Arakawa et al. 2021** (*J Oral Rehabil*, PMID 32799377): pooled tongue
>   pressure **51.9 kPa**; **Wu et al. 2020** measured anterior tongue
>   **56.0 kPa**, posterior **53.2 kPa**. These are pressures, not the gram
>   figures the wiki invented.
> - **Li et al. 2021** (*Int J Oral Sci*, PMID 34183652): optimal force for
>   tooth movement is **28-44 g for tipping**, **110-137 g for translation**.
> - The "500-1000 g" figure appears to be a confusion with **bite force of
>   500-700 *newtons*** — off by a factor of ~1000.
>
> **The AAO position is stronger than this page states.** Press release
> 2024-01-22, AAO President Myron Guymon: *"There's no scientific evidence to
> support its claims of reshaping the jawline, and the potential risks outweigh
> any unproven benefits."* The AAO names specific harms — tooth loosening, bite
> disruption, tooth wear, speech impairment. Their 2025 consumer article states
> *"there's no current research that suggests the technique provides any benefit
> to your jawline or oral health."* The AAO also notes that **John Mew lost his
> orthodontic license over unsupported claims** — a fact missing from every
> page in this wiki.

### The Verdict by Age

| Age | Effectiveness | Notes |
|-----|---------------|-------|
| 8-16 | Potentially helpful | Sutures still open, can influence growth |
| 16-18 | Limited | Some plasticity remains |
| 18+ | Minimal | Bone largely fixed, soft tissue changes only |
| 25+ | Negligible | Focus on fat loss, posture, grooming |

### What Mewing Actually Does in Adults
1. Improves nasal breathing
2. Reduces mouth breathing
3. Lifts hyoid bone (better neck profile)
4. Tightens under-chin area
5. Better resting facial posture

**NOT**: Dramatic bone restructuring

### Mewing Protocol (From looksmax.org)
1. Entire tongue on roof (focus on back third)
2. Teeth lightly touching
3. Lips sealed naturally
4. Nose breathing only
5. Chin tucked, neck straight

**Soft mewing** (light pressure) > Hard mewing (risky)

> **Verified 2026-10-14 — keep the 80%, drop the 90%.** This wiki contained two
> conflicting numbers. The 80% figure traces to **Flament, Bazin & Laquièreze
> 2013**, *Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol* 6:221-232 (DOI 10.2147/CCID.S44686),

> photo-protected vs. photo-exposed Caucasian skin, so it does not generalize.
>

> Baker & Green 2013, *Ann Intern Med* 158:781-790 (PMID 23732711) — 903 adults,
> 4.5 years — daily SPF 45 gave **24% less skin aging** than discretionary use
> (relative odds 0.76, 95% CI 0.59-0.98). Twenty-four percent, not ninety.

> **Verified 2026-10-14 — the "collagen drinks" line is wrong and has been

> **Verified 2026-10-14:**
>
> **Dairy.** The AAD position is that evidence is *suggestive, not established*,
> and it does not recommend universal dairy elimination. **Skim milk
> specifically** carries the strongest and most consistent signal in
> meta-analyses of observational studies; full-fat milk and yogurt are weaker
> and inconsistent, and **whey protein evidence is much weaker still**. A
> 4-8 week personal trial of cutting skim milk is reasonable; blanket dairy
> elimination based on the previous wording is not supported.
>
> **Zinc.** Oral zinc does have genuine (modest) RCT evidence for mild-moderate
> inflammatory acne as an *adjunct*. But **30-40 mg/day is not a target — 40
> mg/day is the NIH tolerable upper limit for adults.** Chronic intake at that
> level causes **copper deficiency**, anemia, and immunosuppression. Do not
> combine with any other zinc supplement.
>
> **Fish oil.** 2-3 g combined EPA+DHA is the standard therapeutic
> anti-inflammatory dose and matches most trials, but it is prescribed for
> inflammatory skin disease — not established for "skin health" generally. High
> doses (≥3 g) raise bleeding risk.
