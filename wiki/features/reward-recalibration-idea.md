# Reward Recalibration Idea (Natural Nootropic for Habit Re-weighting)

## Original idea (2026-09-28)

A natural nootropic that makes bad habits feel *less* rewarding ("bad dopamine") and makes hard work feel *more* rewarding ("good dopamine").

## Sharpened version

Dopamine is not good/bad — it encodes salience and motivation. The actual mechanism:

- Cheap rewards (phone, junk food, porn, doomscrolling) → large phasic spikes → **D2 receptor downregulation** → tolerance → compulsive loop.
- Downregulated D2 in nucleus accumbens → **effort feels not worth it** → hard work loses its reward.
- Therefore the goal is **widening the gap between effort-value and cheap-reward value** (effort-based motivation / reward sensitivity tuning), not splitting dopamine by valence.

## Two-lever protocol

**Lever A — suppress cheap reward:**
1. Friction engineering: phone in another room during work; notifications off (phone-on-desk impairs concentration even when powered off).
2. Sleep 8–9h (restriction increases distractibility AND flattens reward sensitivity).
3. **NAC (N-acetylcysteine) 600–2400 mg/day** — glutamate homeostasis; RCT evidence in addiction/compulsive behavior for reducing craving. Closest pharmacological match to "make bad habits less rewarding."
4. Omega-3 (DHA 1–2 g) as foundational substrate.

**Lever B — amplify effort reward:**
1. **Exercise 3–5×/week, low-moderate intensity** — largest measured natural D2-receptor upregulation + BDNF; biggest cognitive gain available to a teen (SMD 0.42 general cognition).
2. Cold exposure — large sustained dopamine rise without spike-and-crash (Šrámek 2000, n=11; treat as low-risk medium evidence).
3. Caffeine 100–200 mg + L-theanine 100–200 mg — acute, only before deep blocks.
4. Immediate micro-reward after each 90-min block (tick sheet, small celebration) — pairs effort with payoff; conditioning does what no pill can.
5. One written if-then plan before each block: 32% → 71% goal completion (Gollwitzer & Brandstätter 1997).

## Explicitly rejected

- **Mucuna pruriens** ("natural L-DOPA") — a dopamine drug in a plant costume; causes receptor downregulation, would worsen the problem.
- Claiming any supplement does this *selectively* — no natural nootropic has that evidence.

## Honest ceiling

- Pill = ~30% of the effect; behavior layer (friction + if-then + immediate reward) = the rest.
- Tier 1 evidence: exercise, sleep, caffeine+theanine, creatine, omega-3.
- Tier 2 (suppress side): NAC, saffron (snacking/craving RCTs), sulforaphane (early, animal-heavy).
- Habit automaticity: median 66 days, range 18–254 (Lally 2010).
