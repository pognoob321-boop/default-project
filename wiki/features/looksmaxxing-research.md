## Facial Anatomy & Bone Structure

### Key Bones
- **Maxilla** (upper jaw): Continues growing until ~17-21 in males
- **Mandible** (lower jaw): Growth plates close by early-mid 20s
- **Zygomatic bones** (cheekbones): Frame the midface
- **Ramus**: Vertical portion of mandible - critical for jawline definition

### Facial Proportions (Golden Ratio)
- Jaw width to cheekbone width relationship
- Lower third of face balance
- Facial symmetry
- Sexual dimorphism markers

### What Affects Bone Structure
1. **Genetics** (primary factor)
2. **Mechanical stress** from chewing
3. **Hormonal influences** (testosterone, growth hormone, thyroid)
4. **Nutrition** (calcium, vitamin D, K2)
5. **Sleep quality** and breathing patterns
6. **Tongue posture** during development (childhood)

### Age Considerations
- **Under 18**: Mewing/oral posture may influence growth
- **18-25**: Some malleability remains
- **25+**: Bones largely fixed; non-surgical changes minimal
- ~~Adult bone remodeling: ~10% per year~~ **← CONFLATES TURNOVER WITH SHAPE**

> **Corrected 2026-10-14.** The ~10%/year figure is real but it is a **bone
> mass turnover** figure, not a bone *shape* change. Mature adult skeleton
> replaces roughly 5-10% of its mass per year (Schini et al. 2022, *Endocr Rev*
> 44:417 gives ~5%/yr) — but that is net-zero resorption and rebuild. It says
> nothing about whether a jaw will get longer or wider. Kenkre & Bassett 2018
> (*J R Soc Med*, doi:10.1177/0004563218759371) document the widespread
> "bone remodeling improves with age" misconception directly.
>
> Practical meaning: facial bone **shape** is effectively fixed after growth.
> Masseter *muscle* hypertrophy is still achievable at any age; adding *bone*
> is not.

## Mewing: The Science

### What It Is
- Named after Dr. John Mew and Dr. Mike Mew (British orthodontists)
- Proper tongue posture: entire tongue flat against roof of mouth
- Lips sealed, nasal breathing only

### The Claims
- Reshapes jawline and midface
- Improves forward growth
- Enhances cheekbone definition

### The Evidence
**For (limited):**
- Tongue posture does influence childhood facial development
- Oral posture and nasal breathing are real orthodontic principles
- May improve nasal breathing and resting face posture
- Free and risk-free

**Against (stronger):**
- AAO (American Association of Orthodontists) does not recommend mewing
- No credible evidence for adult bone reshaping
- ~~Tongue pressure estimated at 5-10g vs. 500-1000g needed for skeletal change~~ **← INVENTED**
- Before/after photos mostly due to lighting, angles, weight loss
- Adult cranial sutures are fused

> **Corrected 2026-10-14 — the tongue-pressure figures are fabricated.**
> Measured values: **51.9 kPa** pooled (Arakawa 2021, PMID 32799377); anterior
> tongue 56.0 kPa, posterior 53.2 kPa (Wu 2020, PMID 33126580). Optimal
> tooth-movement force is **28-44 g tipping, 110-137 g translation** (Li 2021,
> PMID 34183652). The "500-1000 g" figure appears to confuse gram-force with
> **bite force of 500-700 newtons** — off by roughly 1000x.
>
> **Also missing everywhere in this wiki:** the AAO notes that **John Mew lost
> his orthodontic license over unsupported claims**, and names concrete harms
> — tooth loosening, bite disruption, tooth wear, speech impairment.
> AAO President (2024-01-22): *"There's no scientific evidence to support its
> claims of reshaping the jawline, and the potential risks outweigh any unproven
> benefits."*

### Verdict
- **Children/teens**: Potentially helpful during development
- **Adults**: Won't reshape bone, but improves breathing and resting posture
- **Worth doing**: Yes, as a baseline habit (free, safe)
- **Expectations**: Don't expect dramatic transformation

## Wolff's Law & Bonesmashing

### Wolff's Law (Legitimate Science)
- Bone adapts to loads placed upon it
- Controlled mechanical stress → bone becomes stronger
- Applies to muscular contraction during normal activity

### Bonesmashing (Dangerous Misapplication)
- Hitting face with hammers/blunt objects
- Claimed to create micro-fractures that heal stronger
- **Reality**: Produces contusions, microfractures, nerve damage, facial fractures
- No scientific support for productive remodeling from blunt trauma
- TikTok "bone smashing tutorial" videos: 250M+ views

### Medical Consensus
- Wolff's Law describes gradual remodeling under controlled physiologic loading
- NOT blunt-force trauma
- Dangerous and ineffective

> **Corrected 2026-10-14.** This page previously claimed "SPF 30+ prevents 90% of
> visible aging." **That figure has no source** — it traces to Skin Cancer

> **Hughes et al. 2013**, *Ann Intern Med* 158:781-790 (PMID 23732711): 903

### Why Most "Mewing Results" Are Fat Loss
- Losing facial fat dramatically changes definition
- Often falsely attributed to mewing alone
- ~~BMI decrease of ~2.38 kg/m² needed for noticeable attractiveness improvement~~ **← WRONG ON 3 COUNTS**

> **Corrected 2026-10-14.** Verified directly against Re & Rule 2016, "Heavy
> Matters: The Relationship Between Just Noticeable Differences in Perceptions
> of Facial Adiposity and Facial Attractiveness," *Soc Psychol Pers Sci*
> 7(1):69-76. The previous citation was wrong three ways:
>
> 1. **Wrong sex.** 2.38 kg/m² is for **women**. For **men the figure is
>    2.59 kg/m²** (~18 lbs at average male height vs ~14 lbs for women).
> 2. **Wrong label.** 2.38/2.59 is the threshold needed to shift *attractiveness*
>    judgments. The separate **1.33 kg/m²** figure is the threshold for
>    perceiving *any* weight change in the face — the two are different
>    quantities and the wiki conflated them.
> 3. **Wrong framing.** These are *perceptual detection thresholds* from
>    forced-choice rating tasks, not "the amount of weight loss that improves
>    your looks."
>
> For an adult man of average height, the real numbers are roughly 9 lbs to look
> noticeably different, and about 18 lbs before raters find you more attractive.
