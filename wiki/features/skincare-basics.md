# Skincare Basics

## Core 4 Products

### 1. Cleanser (PM only)
- CeraVe Hydrating Cleanser
- Gentle, non-stripping
- Removes dirt, oil, and sunscreen buildup

### 2. Moisturizer (AM + PM)
- CeraVe Moisturizing Cream
- Restores the skin barrier and prevents dryness
- Induces dryness + irritation from active ingredients

### 3. Sunscreen (AM)
- La Roche-Posay Anthelios
- Prevents sun damage, dark marks, and premature aging
- SPF 30-50 every day

### 4. Retinoid (PM only)
- Tretinoin (prescription) or adapalene (OTC)
- Anti-aging, acne, texture, and long-term skin quality
- Start slow: 2-3x/week, then build up

## How to Use
- AM: cleanser -> moisturizer -> sunscreen
- PM: cleanser -> retinoid (wait 20 min) -> moisturizer

## What Actually Works (Backed by Science)

### Retinol (Anti-aging)
- Boosts collagen and cell turnover
- Improves fine lines, wrinkles, texture, and firmness
- Helps prevent and treat acne

### BHA / Salicylic Acid (Acne)
- Oil-soluble exfoliant that penetrates pores
- Clears acne, blackheads, and whiteheads
- Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant
- ⚠️ **Not the right tool for closed comedones** — the only salicylic acid RCT
  in AAD 2024 found *no difference in closed comedones* vs vehicle at 12 weeks
  (11% better on open comedones, 25% better on inflammatory lesions). Use a
  retinoid for whiteheads. See [Closed Comedones](acne-treatment.md).

### Niacinamide (Brightening)
- Reduces redness, blotchiness, and oiliness
- Fades post-acne marks (PIH/PIE)
- Regulates oil production
- Gentle enough for all skin types

### Vitamin C (Antioxidant)
- Brightens and fades dark spots
- Protects against free radical damage
- Best used in the morning under sunscreen

## Best Add-On: Azelaic Acid
- Reduces redness and inflammation
- Fades post-acne marks
- Helps with texture and uneven tone
- Gentle enough for sensitive skin

## Mostly Hype / Waste of Money
- ~~Collagen (topical) - can't bind to collagen~~ **← OVERSTATED, see note**
- Pore strips - temporarily rip out gunk, come back
- Charcoal mask - mostly marketing
- Essential oils - can irritate skin
- Niacinamide (high dose) - higher isn't better

> **Verified 2026-10-14.** The topical-collagen line is *directionally* right but
> overreaching: topical peptides genuinely cannot penetrate to the dermis and
> re-polymerize into native collagen — that part is established. But "cannot
> replace dermal collagen" ≠ "does nothing." Topical hydrolyzed collagen has
> plausible and partially demonstrated activity as a **humectant and signal
> peptide** at the stratum corneum.
>
> **Separately — oral collagen is a different question and the wiki gets it
> wrong elsewhere.** The companion claim that "collagen drinks are a waste
> because the stomach breaks it down" is a category error: hydrolyzed peptides
> survive digestion. Meta-analysis of 26 RCTs, n=1721 (Pu et al. 2023,
> *Nutrients*, PMID 37432180) found significant gains in hydration and
> elasticity — though much of that literature is industry-funded with high
> heterogeneity (I² up to 90%).
>
> On **niacinamide**, the "higher isn't better" instinct is right but the
> ceiling is softer than this page implies: 2-5% is often better tolerated than
> 10% for barrier and pigmentation work, and 12-20% is used clinically without
> disaster. 10% is a reasonable default, not a hard limit.

## Simple Routine

### Morning
1. Gentle cleanser
2. Vitamin C
3. Optional azelaic acid
4. Hyaluronic acid
5. Moisturizer
6. Sunscreen

### Night
1. Gentle cleanser
2. Azelaic acid (alternate nights)
3. Tretinoin (2-3x/week)
4. Hyaluronic acid (if needed)
5. Moisturizer
