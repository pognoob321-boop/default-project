# Haircut Spec: Korean-Style Curtain Fringe

> A barber-ready spec for the target cut, derived from 5 reference frames.
> **Evidence for whether hair helps: [Hair & Facial Hair Attractiveness Evidence](../research/hair-facial-hair-attractiveness.md)**
> (hair restoration = d = 0.67 for attractiveness — one of the largest effects in this wiki).
>
> ⚠️ **There is zero research on whether this specific cut is attractive on men.**
> Male hairstyle attractiveness is effectively unstudied. This page is a *translation* spec so the
> cut gets communicated correctly — not an effectiveness claim.

## What this cut actually is

Not a buzz, not a skin fade. It is a **curtain fringe + tapered sides + nape-length back**.

Common names it gets described as: curtain fringe, Korean/Japanese bangs, see-through bangs,
French crop (if the back is shorter), curtain mullet (if the back is longer).

## The five parts

| Part | Spec |
|---|---|
| **Part** | Slightly off-centre, about 1-2 cm from middle |
| **Bangs** | Swept forward off the part, fall on both sides of the forehead, ends taper to wispy, tips land around eyebrow level or just below |
| **Top** | Kept long — roughly 10-12 cm — so it styles **both** back and forward |
| **Sides** | Tapered down from the temple, short around and in front of the ear, ear fully exposed. **Low taper, not a skin fade** — keep it dark |
| **Back** | Left long to the nape, roughly 8-10 cm, natural wave. Can be tucked under or left flaring |

## Why it works as a look (mechanics, not research)

- The long top + short sides creates **contrast**, which reads as intentional styling rather than
  a uniform helmet.
- The exposed ear and clean temple line make the head shape read more defined.
- The centre-ish part softens the upper face and brings the eye line forward.
- The wispy tapered fringe frames the eyes without hiding them — this is the part that most affects
  how attractive a face reads at conversation distance.

## How to ask for it

Most Cambodian barbers understand these English terms. **Show the photo** — it is worth more than
any description:

> "Korean style. Curtain bangs in front, parted a little off-centre, ends thin. Keep the top long so
> I can push it back. Fade the sides low and tight — not shaved, I can still see hair. **Leave the
> back long**, down to the neck, don't taper it out."

Key phrases that make or break it:
- **"Low fade, not shaved"** — the single most common mismatch.
- **"Leave the back long"** — barbers default to tapering the back.
- **"Thin the ends of the bangs"** — without this you get a heavy curtain that hides the forehead.

## Styling

- **Forward** (the reference look): light gel or mousse, scrunch, let the curtain fall. Needs a matte
  or low-gel product — shine reads wet.
- **Back**: same product, push back with fingers, no brush. Keeps the volume seen in the reference.
- **The crown whorl** in the reference is a real growth pattern — visible in the top-down frame.
  It is not a styling mistake and cannot be removed.
- Reference the shine finding from [the research page](../research/hair-facial-hair-attiveness.md):
  **shine and straight alignment read as healthier and more attractive.** Not volumise to
  "look bigger" — the one study on volume found high volume *hurt* attractiveness ratings in
  front view (female targets only, but the safer default anyway).

## Growth-out projection

Recorded 2026-10-17 from the actual cut delivered (see "What was actually cut").

Scalp hair grows ~3 mm/week, ~1 cm/month. The critical consequence for this
specific cut: **the top and the sides grow at the same rate**, so the
contrast that makes the cut work shrinks continuously. The cut does not get
worse gradually in every part — the **fringe dies first**, because feathered
and tapered ends are exactly the hair that regrows thickest and least
separated. The see-through quality is a property of the *tips*, and the tips
are the first thing the barber removed.

| Week | Growth | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | +1 cm | **Best window.** Temple fills with visible dark hair, so the near-skin section that read as a recession stops doing that — this is the one thing that gets *better*. Fringe ~1 cm heavier but tips still separated. Contrast still strong. |
| **6–8** | +1.5–2 cm | Curtain starts failing. Tips re-solidify into a mass, fringe sits on the brow, forehead disappears. Top-vs-side contrast noticeably weaker; head silhouette rounds out. Fine day to day, no longer photogenic. |
| **12** | +3 cm | Fringe is a bowl. Sides are 1.5–2 cm with **no visible taper line at all** — the mid-taper mismatch self-corrects by simply disappearing, but the low-taper shape the spec asks for is gone. Back is bushy and flares past the neckline. This is the "I need a cut" point. |

**Shelf life: 6–8 weeks.** Photograph the cut at week 4 — that is the peak.

Trim plan that follows from the above:

1. Week 4 — bangs only, re-shaped **thinner, not shorter**, plus a neckline tidy. Leave the sides.
2. Week 8–10 — sides and back. This is where the taper is rebuilt, and it is
   also the moment to say *low* explicitly.

## What was actually cut (2026-10-17, delivered)

Compared against the five-part spec above. Four of five landed.

| Part | Spec | Delivered |
|---|---|---|
| Part | slightly off-centre | ✓ |
| Bangs | swept, wispy tips at brow level | ✓ heavier than spec |
| Top | long, styles both ways | ✓ |
| Sides | **low taper, dark, not a skin fade** | ✗ **mid taper, near-skin at temple** |
| Back | nape-length, left long | ✓ but neckline flares unevenly |

The one miss is the spec's own flagged-as-most-common one. Its effect: the
temple reads as a recession in profile, and the over-long top looks heavy next
to it. On a narrow face with a small lower third, a top-heavy silhouette works
against length — the widest point of the hairstyle ends up at the top of the
skull, where width is not wanted.

Two fixes that need no barber:

1. **Flatten the volume.** Side and back views show the top taller and wider
   than the head underneath. The one study on hair volume found high volume
   *hurt* front-view attractiveness ratings. Blow-dry the top forward and
   slightly down for photos — 60 seconds, matte or low-gel product only.
2. **Trim the nape where it sticks out.** Keep the centre length.

## Maintenance

- Trim every **3-4 weeks** — the curtain loses its shape faster than the rest because the ends
  are tapered on purpose.
- Bangs taper into the fringe, so expect to re-shape them, not just shorten them.

## Pairing note

The reference face is **clean-shaven**. Per Dixson et al. 2016 (N = 8,520), heavy stubble ranked
above clean-shaven on attractiveness. This cut plus heavy stubble sits above the reference, not at it.
