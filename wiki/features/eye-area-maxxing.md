# Eye Area Maxxing — Complete Guide

> Source: [COMPLETE FACE MAXXING SERIES PART 1 EYE AREA](https://looksmax.org/threads/complete-face-maxxing-series-part-1-eye-area.2088691/),
> looksmax.org, OP Askinov, May 18 2026, 112 replies. Retrieved 2026-10-17.
>
> ⚠️ **This is a forum guide, not research.** Anatomy terms (canthal tilt, orbital
> vector, infraorbital rim) are real and clinically used. The *ideal ranges*,
> the *men need high hooding* claims, and all cost estimates are community
> opinion. Items marked ❌ are corrected below.

## The four eye-area levers

Ordered by how much they actually move your face.

1. Infraorbital support (bone) — the single biggest structural lever
2. Canthal tilt (tendon surgery)
3. Brow position + hooding (skeletal + skin)
4. Skin quality and pigmentation (tretinoin, SPF)

---

## Canthal tilt

Angle between the line connecting medial and lateral canthus.

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| PCT (positive) | medial canthus **below** lateral, 2° or more |
| NCT (neutral) | same level, 0–1° |
| Negative canthal tilt | medial canthus **above** lateral, 1° or more |

Forum ideal: **3–7°**. Cross-check against
[facial-ratios](facial-ratios.md) (consensus 5–9°, tight 6–8°) — the forum is
tighter than the wider community range.

- Negative tilt reads as "prey eyes," sad or fatigued, ages worse.
- Men trend closer to neutral because of a higher nasal bridge.
- PCT looks sharper/more angular; a rounder lower lid is read as feminine.

### Canthopexy vs canthoplasty

The thread's most useful correction, and it is clinically sound:

| | Canthopexy | Canthoplasty |
|---|---|---|
| What | Tendon shortened/sutured to a new position | Tendon fully released, trimmed, re-anchored to the orbital rim with permanent suture anchors |
| Stability | **Relapses** when the underlying rim is recessed — sutures lose to tension and tissue creeps back | Significantly more stable, has a real bony fixation point |

If your infraorbital rim is recessed, canthopexy alone is the wrong procedure.

**Canthoplasty mechanics:** 30 min–3 hr. Small incision at the lateral canthus,
release the lower limb of the lateral canthal tendon, re-anchor higher on the
orbital rim. External sutures out at day 5–7. Swelling 3–4 weeks, final settle
3–6 months. No rubbing eyes or contacts for a month.

**Complications the thread lists:** dry eye (tear film disruption), swelling,
bruising, overcorrection that relaxes as tissue heals.

⚠️ **Not in the thread, and material:** lower-lid **retraction and ectropion**
(pull-down of the lid margin, exposing sclera) are the classic canthoplasty
failure modes, along with visible scarring at the canthus, and permanent
truncation of the lid if too much is released. If it is done wrong it can be
harder to fix than the original problem. Get a board-certified oculoplastic
surgeon, not a general plastic surgeon.

## Infraorbital rim & orbital vector

**Infraorbital** = the zone from the bony orbital rim down to the top of the
cheek, where lower-lid fat pads and skin sit.

**Orbital vector** = the relationship between the most forward projection of
the eyeball and the infraorbital rim.

| Vector | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Positive | rim sits forward of the eyeball — good support, fat and skin have bone to rest on |
| Neutral | same plane; fine |
| Negative | eyeball projects forward of the rim — the eye is unsupported |

Negative vector claimed consequences: faster aging, lid-cheek junction drops
earlier, fat herniates visibly, more hollowness.

Recessed infraorbital rim claimed consequences: hollow look, cast shadow →
dark circles, midface flattening (which makes the nose look bigger), worse eye
bags because nothing holds the tissue up.

**Self-check:** put a finger directly under the eye. Bone right there and skin
is paper-thin → your dark circles are pigmentation/thin skin, not bone. That
test comes from a separate looksmax thread and it is the most useful item in
this whole page.

## Softmax: skin around the eye

### Tretinoin
- 0.025% (the thread prefers the low end), rice-grain amount for the whole
  periorbital area, **over moisturizer** (buffering), 1–2×/day
- Apply to the orbital bone skin, not the lid margin; avoid getting it in the eye
- Claimed: cell turnover, collagen I/III upregulation, MMP inhibition, TGF-β
  signalling, tyrosinase downregulation, PIH clearance

The collagen and PIH mechanisms are real and well documented. Two corrections:

- ❌ "Higher concentrations thin the stratum corneum so you don't want them" —
  true short-term, and long-term tretinoin *thickens* the epidermis. The reason
  to start at 0.025% is irritation, not permanent thinning.
- ⚠️ Rice-grain for the *whole* eye area twice daily is a lot. Most derms
  recommend a much smaller amount and starting at night only. Irritant contact
  dermatitis around the eye is the common failure, and it can be worse than the
  problem you were treating.

### Sunscreen
Daily. Non-negotiable — retinoids increase UV sensitivity and PIH is the
endgame if you skip it.

### Niacinamide
2×/day, whole face, don't miss under the eye. Real evidence for hyperpigmentation
and barrier support.

### Hydroquinone ❌ (as prescribed in the thread)
The thread recommends 0.5–1% under the eye daily for 3–4 months, then cycling.

The tyrosinase inhibition is real. The problems:
- Hydroquinone is **banned in OTC skincare in the EU and several other
  jurisdictions**, and in the US it is a prescription (though widely sold OTC
  anyway).
- The "cycle it" advice is correct and important. Prolonged continuous use can
  cause **ochronosis** — a blue-grey permanent discoloration. 3–4 months on, then
  off, is roughly the standard dermatology window.
- Under-eye skin is among the thinnest on the body and the most likely place to
  show irritation and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation from the irritant
  itself.
- ❌ The thread also offers to teach how to dilute it. Do not. That is how
  people end up using an unknown concentration of a drug on the thinnest skin
  on their face.

### ❌ Monobenzone
The thread mentions it as a working alternative. Do not. It is an irreversible
depigmenting agent used on vitiligo; on normal under-eye skin it can produce
**permanent white patches** that do not tan and do not repigment.

### Collagen
Oral, topical, or injected. Topical peptides have modest evidence for skin
hydration and firmness; they do not rebuild orbital bone.

### Gua sha
One claim per day. Weak evidence; mechanistically it's a temporary change in
perfusion and fluid. Not harmful, not a substitute for anything.

## Upper eye area: brow, hooding, lashes

### Brow position
- Outer end should **not** sit lower than the inner end; neutral or slightly up
  is ideal. Negative brow tilt reads as "prey."
- **Low-set brow** (close to the eye, maximum space between) = masculine.
  **High-set brow** = youthful/feminine, not ideal for men.
- Goal for men: **low set**.

The space between brow and lid crease is the metric to judge this, not the
position relative to the eyeball.

### Supraorbital projection
The brow ridge. Robust and masculine; it also sets where the brow sits.

### Brow fat pad
Sits between frontalis and the orbital rim. Deflates and descends with age. It
is what keeps a youthful, un-hooded upper lid. Age-related loss here is a fat
volume problem, not a skin problem.

### Upper eyelid exposure (UEE)
PSL-community term, not a medical one. Sclera visible above the iris. The
thread's claim is that men benefit from **high hooding** and that more UEE looks
worse. This is the thread's aesthetic preference stated as a rule — treat it as
opinion, though there is literature that excessive scleral show reads as a
tired or startled expression.

### Eyelashes
Thread's claim: dense and thick beats long. Long lashes increase visual contrast
which **draws attention to whatever UEE you have**. Consistent with that,
curling and mascara are legitimate softmax. See
[eyebrow-eye-area](eyebrow-eye-area.md) for Latisse/bimatoprost.

## Lower eye area procedures

| Procedure | What it does | Thread's cost (US) | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| HA filler | fills volume loss, dark circles | $800–1,800 | Yes — hyaluronidase dissolves it |
| Fat grafting | fat from thigh/abdomen/flanks, purified, layered in | $3,500–5,500 | No, but revisable |
| Lower blepharoplasty | removes saggy skin | $3,500–8,500 | No |
| Infraorbital-malar implant | builds bone forward, permanently | $6,500–9,500 standard; $15,000–30,000+ custom | Surgical removal |
| Canthoplasty | raises the outer canthus | not listed | No |

### HA filler — reversible but not risk-free
30 min, numbing cream, mild class volume loss. Thread lists bruising, swelling,
lumps, and **Tyndall effect** (bluish tint from placing filler too shallow).

⚠️ **Missing from the thread: vascular occlusion.** Filler injected into a vessel
under the eye can block blood flow and cause **skin necrosis or blindness**. The
infraorbital and angular arteries are in the treatment zone. Choose a practitioner
who has documented experience with the infraorbital rim, and know that
hyaluronidase is the reversal for the embolus — which is an argument for HA over
anything permanent.

### Fat grafting
Small amounts only; lower eye is a thin region. Own tissue, so no allergy risk.
Not as potent as an implant. The thread notes 20–50% resorption and repeated
sessions; clinical literature generally reports higher resorption than that,
often around 50%+. So: budget for more than one session or expect a partial
result.

### Lower blepharoplasty
General or local. Marks and removes excess lax skin. ~1 hr total, 10–20 min per
eye, 2 weeks to recover, ~3 months for scars to settle.

Thread's listed risks: swelling, bruising, blurry vision, light sensitivity,
numbness, **hollowness if too much skin is removed**, infection.

⚠️ Add: **lower-lid retraction / ectropion** — the classic complication when
the lid-cheek junction is unsupported or when skin support is removed too
aggressively. This is exactly the failure mode of a recessed infraorbital rim,
so the two problems feed each other. Lower blepharoplasty for hollowness
without support can make the hollow look permanent.

### Infraorbital-malar implants
The hardmaxx endpoint for a negative orbital vector. Pushes the lower lid area
forward to match or exceed eyeball projection.

Materials:
- **Silicone** — smooth, highly customizable, does *not* integrate with tissue,
  so easiest to remove or adjust
- **Porous polyethylene (Medpor)** — micropores let blood vessels and tissue
  grow in, locking it in place but making removal very difficult
- **PEEK** — bone-density-matching, used for 3D-printed custom implants

Technique: general anesthesia, ocular plastic or ophthalmic surgeon. Incision
hidden **inside the mouth** (under the upper lip) or inside the lower lid, so no
external scar. A pocket is created over the bone, the implant is slid in and
positioned over the lower rim and cheekbone, then fixed with tiny **titanium
micro-screws** to prevent migration. Recovery 2–3 weeks; final result 3–6 months.

⚠️ Risks not fully covered in the thread: infection and extrusion, palpable or
visible implant edges, migration if not secured, erosion into the bone,
injury to the infraorbital nerve (numbness or neuralgia), and permanent
over-correction that then requires revision surgery.

### Orbital decompression ❌ for cosmetic use
The thread is actually right about this one. It removes/grinds orbital bone to
create space for the eyeball. It is the treatment for **thyroid eye disease
(proptosis)**, a real medical condition, and a high-risk orbital surgery. Using
it to get "hunter eyes" is what the thread calls "utter cope." The thread names
Frank Tufano as the surgeon whose orbital decompression cases went wrong.

## Eye separation: IPD and ES ratio

- **IPD** (interpupillary distance) normal adult range: 60–70 mm. A wide brow
  makes your IPD *look* wider.
- **ES ratio** = eye separation relative to bizygomatic width. Two people with
  identical IPD can look completely different depending on face width. A
  narrower face makes the same IPD read as wide-set.
- **Nasal bridge** drives apparent spacing: a defined projecting bridge pulls
  the eyes *closer together*; a flat bridge spreads them.

### Cosmetic levers (no surgery needed)
1. Eyebrow grooming and makeup
2. Contouring the nose bridge and the medial canthus
3. Rhinoplasty, if the bridge is genuinely the cause
4. Wider zygomatics and jaw improve ES ratio *without changing IPD*

### Glasses fraud
- Eyes look **too close**: pick a clear acetate bridge or a very thin metal
  bridge, with outer corners extending slightly past the temples. Draws the
  viewer's eye outward.
- Eyes look **too wide apart**: thick dark plastic with a **keyhole bridge**. The
  bold dark center bridge cuts the space between the eyes in half.

## What the thread got right, and what it got wrong

**Right (clinically grounded):**
- Infraorbital support is the dominant structural variable in the eye area
- The infraorbital rim is the fix target, not the skin — and you can self-check
  it with a finger
- Canthopexy relapses on a recessed rim; canthoplasty is more stable
- Canthal tilt, orbital vector, and infraorbital rim are real surgical/anatomical
  terms of art, not forum inventions
- Tretinoin's collagen and PIH mechanisms, sunscreen, niacinamide
- The canthal-tilt reliability hierarchy from
[craniofacial-claims-fact-check](../research/craniofacial-claims-fact-check.md)

**Wrong or unsupported:**
- ❌ "Higher tretinoin permanently thins the skin" — long-term it thickens it
- ❌ Monobenzone as an under-eye option
- ❌ Hydroquinone concentration-reduction instructions
- ❌ Volufiline / topical "lipofilling" agents for under-eye volume — no RCT.
  Anemarrhena asphodeloides is a cosmetic ingredient with hydration claims; the
  "3x thicker tear trough in 3 weeks" claims elsewhere on the forum are
  self-reports of a **moisturizing** effect, not fat growth. This is the same
  error as castor oil for brows, already killed on
  [eyebrow-eye-area](eyebrow-eye-area.md).
- ❌ "Higher concentration of a depigmenting drug is better" — the dose-response
  runs the wrong way for safety
- ⚠️ "Men need high hooding" is aesthetic preference stated as biology
- ⚠️ Every cost figure is a forum quote with no sourcing
