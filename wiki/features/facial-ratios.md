# Facial Ratios & Measurements

> ## ⚠️ READ THIS BEFORE USING ANY NUMBER BELOW
>
> **Verified 2026-10-14: this table is looksmax.org community consensus, not
> research.** It was audited against the primary literature and the values map
> **verbatim onto forum threads**, not onto any peer-reviewed paper. No study
> derived these ranges. Full audit:
> [Looksmaxxing Attractiveness Claims Audit](../research/looksmaxxing-attractiveness-claims-audit.md).
>
> ### Three things to know
>
> **1. Population norm ≠ attractiveness ideal.** These are different quantities
> and this page conflates them everywhere. The cleanest example:
> - Ideal male nasolabial angle: **93.4-98.5°** (Armijo et al. 2012, *PRS*,
>   PMID 22090249)
> - Population **norm**: **114 ± 10°** (Fitzgerald et al. 1992, *AJODO*)
>
> That is a **~15° gap**. If you optimize toward the "ideal" and your face is
> already at the norm, you are making it worse.
>
> **2. Which numbers have any basis at all**
>
> | Ratio | Status |
> |-------|--------|
> | **ESR ~46% of bizygomatic** | **Independently supported.** Pallett, Link & Lee 2010 (*Vision Res*, PMID 19896961) found optimal interocular distance = 46% of face width. Coincidental agreement with the forum — not derived from it. |
> | Canthal tilt 6-8° | Below the measured **population mean of 8.45 ± 3.53°** (Lee et al. 2020, *J Korean Assoc Oral Maxillofac Surg*, n=76). No empirically-derived optimum exists. |
> | FWHR 1.9-2.06 | No scientific basis as an *ideal*. It is a real measure but its use here is as a dominance/testosterone signal — unsettled, with replication problems. |
> | Gonial 112-125° | Roughly matches the **population norm** (~124 ± 6° M), but a norm is not an aesthetic target. |
> | tFWHR 1.33-1.38 | **No literature at all.** Not a standard anthropometric or aesthetic measure. |
> | Chin-to-philtrum 2.1-2.5 | **No study validates this as an attractiveness determinant.** |
> | Midface ratio 0.98-1.02 | **No published definition or norm** for this as an attractiveness target. |
>
> **3. Deleted as unsupported by the science**
> - **Golden ratio (1.618)** — not the optimum. Pallett et al. 2010 found
>   preferred ratios are *significantly different* from phi (t(2) = -8.63,
>   p<.05); their "new golden ratios" are just average-face proportions.
>   Green 1995 and Zwahlen et al. 2022 concur. **The golden ratio does not
>   predict facial attractiveness.**
> - **"Third eyeball rule"** — no peer-reviewed validation. looksmax.org's own
>   thread concedes it is "flawed and incorrect."
> - **Facial fifths as an ideal** — Farkas et al. 1985 found the nose-width
>   canon held in only **37%** of young adults. Equal thirds and fifths are
>   ideals, not norms; Farkas explicitly warned against reading the canons as
>   prescriptions.
>
> ### What actually predicts attractiveness (real research)
> - **Averageness** — d = 0.52 across 18 papers (Rhodes 2006)
> - **Symmetry** — r = 0.30 rated / 0.19 measured (Rhodes 2006); largely a
>   *health cue* that disappears when perceived health is controlled
> - **Sexual dimorphism** — weak-to-null in modern large-N work. See the
>   "masculinity paradox" (Dixson et al. 2016, n=8520): both masculinized and
>   feminized faces were rated *less* attractive than unmodified faces.
>
> **Bottom line: use this page to understand the community's vocabulary, not to
> set goals. If a number here matters to you, check it against a real
> anthropometric norm first.**

## Core Width / Height

### tFWHR (1.33–1.38)
- Total facial width-to-height: bizygomatic width ÷ face height (hairline→chin)
- Replaces older "TFWR 1.31–1.34" label

### FWHR (1.9–2.06)
- Facial width-to-height: bizygomatic width ÷ (mid-eyebrow → upper lip height)
- Newest tight band: 1.95–2.05 (Definitive Guide)
- Legacy floor: 1.8+ (2019 BOTB)
- Heavily cited link to dominance / testosterone / aggressiveness

### Bigonial Width (85.5–92% of bizygomatic)
- Gonion-to-gonion ÷ cheekbone-to-cheekbone
- Newest tight band: 87.5–91.5% (Definitive Guide)
- Implies bizygomatic:bigonial ratio ≈ 1.35

### Bitemporal Width (84–95% of bizygomatic)
- Newest: 85–92%

### Neck Width (90–100% of bigonial)
- Newest: 92–98% relative to bigonial width

### Face Length / Height-Width (1.33–1.37×)
- Total face length ÷ bizygomatic width

## Vertical Proportions

### Facial Thirds (each 29.5–36.5%; newest ideal split below)
- Upper third: 30–32%
- Middle third: 31.4–33.4%
- Lower third: 33.9–37.0%
- Classic rule: 1:1:1 (hairline → brows → nose base → chin)
- 500-pt harmony guide ideal: U 33% / M 35% / L 33%

### Lower Third Proportion (30.6–34% of total face height)
- Newest: ~34–37% (Definitive Guide)
- Nostril→commisure: 31–33.5% of lower-third height

### Midface Ratio (0.93–1.01)
- IPD ÷ (nasion → upper lip height); closer to 1.0 the better
- Newest tight: 0.98–1.02
- Legacy BOTB: 1.0–1.1

### Chin-to-Philtrum Ratio (2.05–2.55)
- Newest: 2.1–2.5
- Legacy BOTB: 2.0–2.25

### Lower-to-Upper Lip Ratio (1.4–2)
- Legacy single-point ideal: 1.62

### Mouth-to-Nose Ratio (1.38–1.53)
- Newest: 1.4–1.6
- Legacy: 1.5–1.62

## Eye Area

### ESR — Eye Separation Ratio (44.3–47.4%)
- IPD ÷ bizygomatic width × 100
- Newest tight: 45–47%
- "Third eyeball" rule widely considered flawed by these threads

### Eye Spacing / ES (0.93–1.04)
- Inner canthal distance ÷ eye width

### Eye Aspect Ratio / PFL:PHL (2.8–3.6)
- Palpebral fissure length ÷ height
- Newest: 3–3.7×
- Legacy: 3.0–3.5

### Canthal Tilt (5–9° positive)
- Newest: 6–8° positive
- Male Tier-1 in harmony guide: 5–8.5°; female: 6–9.5°
- Measured: medial canthus (vertex) → lateral canthus vs horizontal

## Jaw & Lower Face

### Gonial Angle (112–125°)
- Newest bands: 112–123° (38-ratios) / 113–125° (harmony) / ~115–121° (Definitive)
- Legacy: 115–130°
- Vertex at gonion, arms along mandible body and ramus
- Smaller = sharper/more vertical jaw; larger = softer

### Jaw Frontal Angle (84.5–95°)
- Newest tight: 86.5–92.5° (harmony: 82–94°)

### Mandibular Plane Angle (15–24°)
- Harmony: 15–24°; 38-ratios: 15–22°
- Lower/flatter = sharper horizontal jaw; steeper = long weak recessed look

- Longer ramus preferred with strong vertical jaw height

- Cheekbone position / setness: 81–100% (high, laterally projecting zygos + ogee curve)
- Lower/full face ratio: 0.62+

- Facial fifths: 5 equal horizontal columns (nose, eye fissure, lateral canthus→ear each 1/5)
- PFL:PHL: 3–3.5

## Facial Plane Angles

### 1. Mandibular Plane Angle
- Tilt of lower jaw from the side
- Good: jaw runs forward flat/horizontal → sharp
- Bad: jaw slopes down → long, weak, recessed look
- Ideal: ~15–24° (newer) / ~12.5° below horizontal (older)

### 2. Orbitomaxillary Plane Angle
- Angle of cheekbone / under-eye / maxilla
- Good: cheekbones project outward → facial depth
- Bad: flat midface, unsupported under-eyes, recessed cheeks
- Ideal: ~25° outward

### 3. Temporal Plane Angle
- Side-of-head / temple taper
- Good: temples taper cleanly and flex outward
- Bad: pinched, narrow, awkward upper face
- Ideal: ~10° yaw
