## Two business models, not one

**A. Faceless meme account → merch.** @tdoc22. No face, no persona, no advice. The
product is a joke shirt. Attention is the only asset; the shirt is a margin play.
Reachable with zero on-camera presence.

**B. Face-forward coach → community → subscription.** Cale, Dexpilled, Weatherman.
The video is a free sample; the bio link is a funnel:

```
TikTok video (hook + one tip, 15-45s)
  -> Linktree / Skool / stan.store
    -> free guide or Discord  (lead magnet)
      -> paid Skool membership / course   ($39-99/mo is the observed band)
```

The paid tier is a **community**, not a PDF. Skool is the default venue for this niche.

## Content format (all four)

- 9:16, 15-60s, talking head or meme clip
- One claim per video, delivered as a bold on-screen text hook in the first ~1s
- Pinned videos are the "proof" reel: challenge runs, 30/60/90-day transformations,
  subscriber-count milestones
- High cadence: multiple posts per day is normal for the pinned-grid density seen
- Monetization proof is displayed *in the bio* (Forbes badge, "#1 ranked" claim, follower count)
  — the bio is a landing page, not a description

## Risks

1. **Regulatory.** The category's biggest name, Clavicular (Braden Peters), faces 15
   CSAM charges, a rape charge, and an underage-arrest charge (Times of India 2026-09,
   Global News 2026-09, NY Post 2026-02). Hardmaxxing + a very young male audience is the
   pattern that draws it. Content should stay on evidence-based softmaxxing only.
2. **Claim risk.** This repo's own audit ([Looksmaxxing Verification Pass](../research/looksmaxxing-verification.md))
   found ~15 of ~90 standard claims survive, 2 are actively harmful. Selling the folklore
   version is both a legal and a reputational exposure.
3. **Platform dependency.** All four are single-platform with a link that lives on a
   third-party host (Linktree / Skool / stan.store / Fourthwall) — a ban takes the funnel
   with it. Clavicular returned to TikTok after a two-month suspension.
