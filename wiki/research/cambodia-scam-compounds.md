# Scam Call Centers and Online Fraud in Cambodia

## Summary

Cambodia has become a global epicenter for cyber-enabled fraud, particularly "pig butchering" cryptocurrency investment scams. The industry generates an estimated $12.5–19 billion annually in Cambodia alone — equivalent to 40–60% of the country's GDP. At peak, 100,000–150,000 people were held in compounds across 86+ identified sites, trafficked from 66+ countries and forced to run scams under threat of violence. Americans lost $21 billion in 2025 alone. The industry is deeply intertwined with Cambodia's political elite, with senators, the PM's cousin, and former PM Hun Sen's advisors directly implicated. Despite unprecedented international pressure and Cambodia's most aggressive crackdown to date, the industry has not been dismantled.

## Scam Types

- **Pig Butchering (Sha Zhu Pan)**: Dominant scam. Fake romantic relationships built over weeks/months, then victims directed to fraudulent crypto investment platforms
- **Crypto/Investment Fraud**: Fake trading platforms mimicking legitimate exchanges
- **Romance Scams**: AI-generated profiles targeting lonely individuals
- **"Digital Arrest" Scams**: Impersonating law enforcement to extort payments
- **Voice Phishing**: AI deepfake voice cloning for real-time impersonation
- **Sextortion**: Soliciting explicit materials for blackmail

AI tools (including ChatGPT) are integral — generating multilingual scripts, creating convincing personas, producing deepfake audio/video.

## Political Connections

- **Chen Zhi** (Prince Group): Personal advisor to Hun Sen and Hun Manet; maintained ledgers of hundreds of millions in bribes; bought a $3M yacht for a senior official; bragged of earning $30 million/day
- **Senator Kok An**: Crown Resorts chain — casinos converted to scam compounds
- **Senator Ly Yong Phat**: O'Smach Resort — 157 buildings, 29 used for scam ops, 10,000+ people
- **Hun To** (PM's cousin): Director of Huione Pay; 30% ownership; laundered $4+ billion
- **Deputy PM Sar Sokha**: Co-founded Jinbei Casino with Chen Zhi; now in charge of anti-scam enforcement
- Amnesty documented complicity between Chinese compound bosses and Cambodian police

## Impact on Victims

**Those forced to scam:**
- Beatings, torture, electric shocks, sexual violence, death
- PTSD, trauma, suicidal ideation
- Arrested as criminals rather than identified as trafficking victims
- After crackdowns, thousands stranded on streets — no money, passport, or shelter

**Those scammed:**
- Americans lost $21 billion in 2025
- Individual victims lost life savings, $400,000+, entire retirement funds
- Conservative global estimate: $64 billion/year stolen (UNODC)

## Assessment

Despite unprecedented international pressure, experts universally agree the industry has not been dismantled. The underlying criminal networks — embedded in Cambodia's political economy and enabled by Chinese organized crime — remain intact. Operations are shifting to smaller urban locations (apartments, shophouses, hotel floors), making detection harder. As one analyst put it: "There is a huge difference between being raided and being shut down."
