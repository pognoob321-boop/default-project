# Gooning: Evidence Review — What Is Actually Bad or Good

Deep-dive on "gooning" (prolonged edging to a trance-like "goon state"), its neuroscience, its real harms, its real benefits, and the clinical framing. Companion to [Gooning/Masturbation/Testosterone Fact-Check](gooning-masturbation-testosterone.md) (2026-09-22), which covered the hormone/sleep arithmetic. This page covers the practice itself, the neuroscience claims, the ED dispute, and treatment.

## 0. The methodological caveat that governs everything else

**There is zero peer-reviewed research on "gooning" as a named practice.** The term has no PubMed literature. Every claim about it is transferred from four adjacent literatures: (a) edging as a sexual technique, (b) masturbation physiology, (c) pornography-use/problematic-use studies, (d) compulsive sexual behaviour disorder (CSBD). Anything you read about gooning specifically — from the community, from clinics, from AI — is extrapolation. Judge it by which of the four literatures it is actually citing.

**Critical distinction.** Gooning ≠ edging ≠ masturbation ≠ porn use ≠ CSBD:

- **Edging** is a short, functional, controllable technique (delay orgasm to extend sex or train against premature ejaculation).
- **Masturbation** is a single event with a completion (orgasm).
- **Gooning** is the *unbounded* version: prolonged, hours to days, aiming to sustain the pre-orgasm state itself. The goal is the arousal state, not the release.
- **CSBD** is a diagnosis about *failed control plus impairment*. It has nothing to do with technique, duration, or frequency as such.

## 2. What's GOOD — evidence-supported

### 2.1 Edging is medically benign
Clinical consumer-health sources converge: edging is generally safe with unlikely detrimental health effects, and the only documented physiological effect is **epididymal hypertension** ("blue balls") — painful, benign, self-resolving.
- Healthline, "Is Edging Bad?" — "deliberately delaying your orgasm — isn't harmful"; also: **edging is not a cause of erectile dysfunction**.
- Medical News Today, 2024 — "generally a safe practice and unlikely to have any detrimental health effects."
- Cleveland Clinic — the only harm cited is chafing/tender skin from roughness.
- **Boundary:** edging ≠ delayed ejaculation disorder. DE is >30 minutes to ejaculate, or inability to ejaculate — a medical condition with vascular/neurologic causes (diabetes, MS), not a consequence of edging.

### 2.2 Masturbation is developmentally normal and health-neutral-to-enhancing
- *Current Opinion in Pediatrics* review (PMC3761219): "contemporary medicine holds masturbation to be developmentally normal, and health-neutral if not health-enhancing."
- Teen prevalence (Robbins 2011, *JAMA Pediatrics*, n=820 nationally representative US 14–17y): 73.8% of males vs 48.1% of females ever masturbated; ever-rate rises 62.6% → ~80% from age 14 → 17; **49.1% of males reported ≥2×/week**. Arnal & Llario 2006: 87.3% of 13–14y boys.
- Counterintuitive finding worth stating: in that cohort, masturbation was associated with **greater condom use and more partnered sexual behaviour**, not less. Authors: "masturbation is an important component of adolescent sexuality rather than an isolated or transient phenomenon."

### 2.3 Orgasm improves perceived sleep
Oesterling et al. 2023, *Journal of Sleep Research* (cross-sectional + longitudinal diary; partnered sex and masturbation each split by with/without orgasm):
- **With orgasm:** perceived shorter sleep latency and better sleep quality in men and women (p<0.001 both).
- **Without orgasm:** perceived longer latency and worse quality (men p<0.002, women p<0.001) — **negative effect strongest in men.**
- Confounder control: alcohol (−0.59) and insomnia severity (−0.65) predicted worse quality, so the sexual-activity effect is not trivially explained.
- **This is the single most direct finding bearing on gooning** — see §3.1.

### 2.4 Ejaculation frequency and prostate cancer risk
- Leitzmann et al. 2004, *JAMA* 291(13):1578–86 — Health Professionals Follow-up Study, n=29,342, 222,426 person-years, 1,449 cases. ≥21 ejaculations/month vs 4–7: RR 0.89 (ages 20–29, NS), 0.68 (ages 40–49), 0.49 (past year), 0.67 lifetime. Each increment of 3 ejaculations/week across a lifetime → 15% lower risk. No association with *increased* risk.
- Leitzmann et al. 2016 — updated +10y, n=31,925, 480,831 person-years, 3,839 cases. HR 0.81 (age 20–29) and 0.78 (age 40–49), p<0.0001 trend. Driven by low-risk disease; held in a PSA-screened cohort.
- Meta-analysis (PMC12794404): pooled OR for higher ejaculation frequency **0.83 (95% CI 0.77–0.90)**, I²=50%.
- **Caveat that matters:** in the same meta-analysis, **masturbation frequency specifically was OR 1.12 (0.82–1.54) — not significant.** The protective signal tracks *ejaculations*, not *masturbation as a method*. Level of evidence 2b. Observational; residual confounding plausible.
- Proposed mechanisms (2025 mechanistic review, PMC11898507): sympathetic suppression, slowed prostate epithelial division, reduced intraluminal crystalloids, endocannabinoid (2-AG) release.

### 2.5 Orgasm neurochemistry (real, and it's the reason it feels good)
- Krüger et al. 2005, *World J Urol* 23(2):130–138 — prolactinergic and dopaminergic mechanisms underlying arousal and orgasm.
- Stoléru et al. 2012, *Neurosci Biobehav Rev* 36(6):1481–1509 (meta-analysis): consistent activation of lateral occipitotemporal, orbitofrontal, mPFC, insula, ACC, frontal premotor cortices plus amygdala, claustrum, hypothalamus, caudate, thalami, cerebellum, substantia nigra. **Ejaculation is associated with decreased activation throughout the prefrontal cortex.** (Same study notes sexual cues activate amygdala/thalamus more in men than women.)
- Melis & Argiolas 1995, *Neurosci Biobehav Rev* 19(1):19–38 — mesolimbic-mesocortical system drives arousal/motivation; nigrostriatal drives sensory-motor copulation; **D2 is the receptor subtype carrying most of the weight** in male sexual behaviour.
- Stress/tension relief: Raymond et al. (via Zhu 2025 review) — 83% of hypersexual men reported decreased mental tension after sexual behaviour. **Caveat: hypersexual patients, not general population.**

### 2.6 A legitimate therapeutic use
Start–stop and squeeze techniques are established behavioural treatments for **premature ejaculation**. That is edging used with a bounded goal and a completion — the exact structural opposite of gooning.

## 3. What's BAD — evidence-supported

### 3.1 The defining feature of gooning is the one variant associated with WORSE sleep
Arousal **without** orgasm is what gooning is; Oesterling 2023 measured that variant as the only one associated with increased sleep latency and decreased sleep quality, most strongly in men. Mechanistically this is coherent: **prolactin rises with orgasm, not with arousal alone.**
- Exton et al. 2001, *Neurosci Behav Rev* 25(8):593–598 — plasma prolactin substantially increased for >1 hour after orgasm (masturbation and coitus), **unchanged after sexual arousal without orgasm.**
- Valente et al. 2021, *Communications Biology* 4:11 — *refutes* the long-standing hypothesis that prolactin causes the post-ejaculatory refractory period. Pharmacologically mimicking the prolactin spike did **not** induce a refractory state; blocking it did **not** shorten one. Conclusion: prolactin release is a side-effect of downstream dopamine/oxytocin/serotonin changes, not the driver.
- **Synthesis:** gooning sustains the "wanting" engine (dopamine) without ever engaging the shutdown (prolactin). Whether that is "harmful" in a measurable way is unproven — but it is the reason the practice is built on never finishing.

### 3.2 Time cost → stolen slow-wave sleep → measurable hormone cost
From the companion page: sessions run "hours, up to days" (Lucas 2022). At that duration the mechanism is not the act, it's the clock:
- Leproult & Van Cauter, *JAMA* 2011;305(21):2173–2174 — 1 week at 5h/night → daytime testosterone **−10 to −15%** in young healthy men.
- Van Cauter & Plat, *J Pediatr* 1996;128(5 Pt 2):S32-7 — **~70% of GH pulses in men coincide with slow-wave sleep**, largest pulse in the first hours of night.
- Any late-night multi-hour session eats directly into that window.

### 3.3 The dopamine-downregulation claim has a direct null PET finding against it
This is the most-repeated mechanistic claim in gooning/NoFap/porn-addiction discourse — "repeated overstimulation downregulates dopamine receptors so you need escalating intensity" — and it is the one that has been directly tested and failed.

**Stormezand et al. 2021, *Eur Neuropsychopharmacol*** (PMID 33774451): [11C]-raclopride PET + arterial-spin-labeling MRI. 15 men with compulsive pornography use vs 10 controls, ages 18–50.
- **No group difference in striatal D2/3 receptor availability.**
- **No correlation between compulsive-use severity and D2/3 availability.**
- No frontal hypoperfusion, no cerebral blood flow difference.
- Verbatim conclusion: *"The current study fails to provide imaging support for sharing similar neurobiological alterations as previously has been reported in other addictive modalities."*

Contrast with substance addictions, where reduced D2/D3 availability **is** replicated: Volkow et al. on cocaine (reduced D2 + frontal metabolism); Lee et al. 2009, *J Neurosci* 29(47):14734–14740 on methamphetamine (reduced striatal D2/3, linked to impulsivity).

So the "chaser effect" and "ΔFosB" stories are extrapolations from drug models to a behaviour nobody has imaged longitudinally. Related: the general "dopamine detox" concept was explicitly debunked as a trend (NLM / The Scientist, 31 July 2024) — fasting from pleasure does not "reset" dopamine.

### 3.4 Erectile dysfunction: the claim is far weaker than the community says

**What does NOT hold up:**
- **Mere porn use → ED.** Zacharopoulos et al. 2026, *Adv Exp Med Biol* 1487:297–304 (PMID 41273571), systematic review, 11 studies 2013–2023: *"mere watching of pornography is not a significant risk factor for developing a sexual dysfunction… frequency of pornography consumption did not seem to be a predictor factor… as much as problematic use."*
- Dwulit & Rzymski 2019, *J Sex Med* (PMID 31247949): *"little if no evidence that pornography use may induce delayed ejaculation and erectile dysfunction."*
- **Causality specifically tested and not found.** Prairie & Lewandowski 2019, *J Sex Med* (PMID 30621919), three samples including a 1-year 4-wave longitudinal (n=433→196) with latent growth curve modelling: problematic use ↔ ED **cross-sectionally**, but *"no significant relationships between any pornography-related variables and trajectories of ED… no evidence of causal links."*
- **The only lab study with actual partners found the opposite of the claim.** Prause & Siegle 2022, *Sex Health* (PMID 35469589): partners viewing more porn showed **greater** arousal to the real partner.
- **Anxiety, not porn, predicts ED — measured directly inside the NoFap population.** Prause & Binnie 2022, *J Psychosex Health* 4(4):252–254, n=669 recruited from Reboot/NoFap/NoFapChristians channels: among those who had done a reboot, anxiety predicted ED (est −0.48, 95% CI −0.82 to −0.13, p=.006). **Porn frequency did not mediate or moderate it.** Powered to detect a slope as small as β=0.05. Men who did reboots reported *more* ED than those who didn't.
- **Engagement with the forums tracks worse outcomes.** Prause et al. 2023, *J Sex Med* 20(Suppl 1) (n=693 survey + 10,971 r/NoFap posts): the most-active forum posters reported the most ED, depression, anxiety, and suicidal ideation at last relapse. Abstract conclusion: *"Treatments that conceptualize pornography as causing erectile dysfunction appears to paradoxically cause distress and erectile problems to worsen."*
- **Preregistered, and blunt.** Prause et al. 2024, "Iatrogenic effects of Reboot/NoFap on public health," *Sexualities*: of 587 men who had done reboots, **28.9% said their most recent failure to maintain the abstinence goal caused them to feel suicidal.** Forums were unlikely to have been used alongside professional help; the authors compared the intervention to iatrogenic illness and recommended no further resource investment in abstinence-based programs. Also cites a 5-year follow-up in which **95% of men with baseline pornography concerns spontaneously recovered.**
- Moral-incongruence confound: Grubbs et al. 2020, *Clin Psychol Sci* 8(5):936–946 — moral incongruence strongly predicts self-reported *problematic* porn use; the "addiction" self-label can be substantially moral disapproval in disguise.

**What does have some support (be fair):**
- Christman et al. 2017 (Naval Medical Center, presented at AUA): IIEF dysfunction in 22% of men who preferred intercourse without porn, 31% with porn, **79% who preferred masturbation with porn**; across all five IIEF domains. Cross-sectional, self-report, uncorroborated causally.
- Kazan et al. 2024, *Int J Impot Res* 36(6):621–626, n=66 young men with organic causes excluded: porn frequency ↔ IIEF r=−0.535 (p<0.001), partially mediated by dyadic adjustment.
- A clinical-review layer reports men whose erectile/arousal difficulties improved after stopping internet porn (Van Eekhout/Van der Wyl/Griffin review). Clinical-report evidence, not trial evidence.

**Verdict.** The *population* evidence does not support "porn made my dick stop working," and the mechanism has never been proposed coherently (Prause: *"No specific mechanism has been proposed that could link pornography viewing alone to sexual arousal responses to a human partner"*). Anxiety is the best-established predictor (Velurajah 2022 systematic review; Calzo 2021; Manalo 2022 — including men under 30), via a documented self-fulfilling-prophecy loop (Beck & Barlow 1986: anxious man misreads normal erection variability → fears failure → anticipates failure → fails). Note also that questionnaire-based ED reporting systematically overstates physiological ED versus RigiScan (Melman et al. 2006) — relevant, because the NoFap population defines ED at a very low threshold and may be catastrophising rather than impaired.

There **is** a real conditioning story for *delayed* ejaculation — which is why start–stop and squeeze exist as PE therapy, and why "death grip" desensitization is a recognised pattern (Healthline: not an official diagnosis, but real; peripheral penile nerves regenerate well; typically reversible over ~3–4 weeks with reduced frequency, relaxed grip, and pelvic floor relaxation). That's a narrow, self-limiting, well-understood phenomenon. It is not the same as "my brain is permanently broken."

### 3.5 Physical harms (real, mostly mundane, mostly reversible)
- **Chafing / tender skin** — Cleveland Clinic; the only harm Cleveland Clinic names for ordinary masturbation.
- **"Death grip" desensitization → delayed ejaculation** — Healthline: not an official diagnosis; hyperstimulation lowering sensitivity is well recognised; perpetuating cycle of needing more force to counter declining sensitivity. Reversible.
- **Prone/mattress masturbation** — case reports of penile numbness and, with extreme force, penile fracture. Clinicians emphasise: dry/rough friction causes skin, not nerve, damage; nerve complaints are usually functional desensitization or performance anxiety.
- **Epididymal hypertension** — benign, resolves.
- **Pelvic floor tension/clenching** — described by clinicians as a contributor to reduced sensitivity and erection quality; addressed with relaxation and reverse-kegel work.
- **Exhaustive-masturbation syndrome** — rare but documented psychiatric presentation after prolonged sessions running into days (Lucas 2022 documents multi-day sessions). Low base rate, high severity.

### 3.6 The only thing that is a disorder: CSBD, and it requires four gates
**ICD-11, Kraus et al. 2018, *World Psychiatry* 17(1):109–110.** Classified as an **impulse-control disorder**, deliberately *not* in the substance-use/behavioral-addiction grouping, because "we do not yet have definitive information on whether the processes involved… are equivalent to those observed in substance use disorders, gambling and gaming."

All must be met:
1. Persistent failure to control intense, repetitive sexual impulses → repetitive behaviour, via ≥1 of: (a) becomes the central focus of life to the point of neglecting health, personal care, other interests/responsibilities; (b) numerous unsuccessful efforts to control or significantly reduce; (c) continues despite adverse consequences (relationship, occupational, health); (d) continues even when deriving little or no satisfaction.
2. **≥6 months' duration.**
3. **Marked distress or significant impairment** in personal, family, social, educational, occupational, or other important functioning.
4. **Distress entirely attributable to moral judgments is explicitly insufficient.** High libido with intact control and no impairment is **not** a disorder. Common adolescent masturbation is **not** a disorder.

- **Prevalence:** German general-population study, n=4,633 — lifetime 4.9% men / 3.0% women. International Sex Survey, n=82,243, 42 countries — ~5% global, no orientation difference. **Only ~14% of people with CSBD seek treatment.** (Older Western estimates 8–13% men / 5–7% women use looser hypersexality criteria and overcount.)
- **Neuroimaging is suggestive, not established.** Voon et al. 2014, *PLOS ONE* 9(7):e102419 — greater dACC, ventral striatum, amygdala response to sexual cues in CSBD vs controls = wanting-without-liking. Gola et al. — heightened activation to cues *predicting* sexual stimuli but **not** to the actual stimuli (incentive-salience account). Klucken et al. — amygdala difference only, **no** ventral striatum difference. Miner et al. — **no** DTI differences. Structural findings conflict (some ↓ anterior cingulate grey matter with symptom severity; some ↑ amygdala volume; one ↑ cerebellar tonsil). Zhu 2025: *"the findings should be interpreted with caution… larger samples and longitudinal designs are necessary."*
- **Comorbidity is high, and direction of causation often runs the other way.** Depression and anxiety most common; >40% also meet criteria for a substance use disorder (alcohol most common); 19.6% of gamblers show CSB; in 70.5% of comorbid cases CSB *preceded* pathological gambling. Bipolar: **31–45% of adolescents with bipolar disorder show CSB during manic episodes, and CSB is prodromal before the first manic episode in 17%** — so mania frequently drives the behaviour rather than the reverse, and CSBD cannot be diagnosed if the behaviour only occurs during mania. Also: elevated OCD, ADHD-symptom (sub-threshold), suicide risk.

### 3.7 "Flatline" — real experience, folklore timeline
NoFap's own FAQ now concedes it: *"The exact brain mechanisms behind the flatline aren't yet known… we have yet to see any peer-reviewed academic research on it."* ~25% of surveyed rebooters report it; timing is unpredictable (any time, any number of times, or never). Springer 2021, *Arch Sex Behav* analysis of r/NoFap abstinence journals: **~one-third reported *diminished* desire during abstinence**, and the flatline was itself a documented trigger — users reset their streak by masturbating to "test whether everything still works." NoFap also states detailed day-by-day flatline timelines found online are pseudoscience.

### 3.8 Shaming and misinformation as iatrogenic harm
- Chasioti & Binnie 2021, *Arch Sex Behav* 50(5):2227–2243 — critical narrative analysis of NoFap/PornFree forums: framing pornography as an "addiction" **maintained clients' distress** about their sexuality.
- Taylor & Jackson 2018, *Sexualities* 21(4):621–639 — "I want that power back": the masculinity discourse converts sexual anxiety into a fight against a drug, protecting self-esteem short-term while entrenching anxiety long-term (Prause 2022).
- Prause et al. 2024 — see §3.4 for the suicidality numbers.

### 3.9 Community and external spillover
A minority subculture ("nut-and-dash," "tagging," "cum sharking") performs public masturbation and then ejaculates on or wipes semen on women, including in Dollar Tree stores. *The Guardian* (20 July 2026) documented a multi-location pattern; civil lawsuits have been filed (San Diego/Clairemont; Colorado) alleging 11+ victims across the US. This is sexual assault and criminal conduct, distinct from the private practice, but it is a documented product of the subculture.

## 4. The actual risk model

The evidence does **not** support "gooning destroys dopamine." It supports a narrower and far more actionable claim:

> **Gooning is medically benign as a technique. The harm is a function of duration × timing × completion × loss of control — nothing else.**

| Lever | Safe zone | Risk zone | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Duration** | Ordinary session, ends with orgasm | Hours; multi-day | Oesterling 2023; Leproult 2011 (sleep cost) |
| **Timing** | Ends well before bedtime; SWS window protected | Runs late, into the 10pm–5:30am SWS window | Leproult JAMA 2011 (T −10–15%); Van Cauter 1996 (~70% GH pulses in SWS) |
| **Completion** | Orgasm reached → prolactin surge, sleep benefit | Arousal without orgasm → no prolactin, worse perceived sleep, strongest in men | Exton 2001; Oesterling 2023; Valente 2021 |
| **Control** | Can stop. Goal has an end. | 6+ months, failed control attempts, continues despite harm and despite no satisfaction, with functional impairment | ICD-11 CSBD criteria (Kraus 2018) |
| **Coupling** | No compulsive porn dependency | Fetishised porn addiction; sessions run to "days" | Monea 2025; Lucas 2022 |

**Only the fourth lever makes it a disorder. That is the one to actually test.**

## 5. Self-screen (the ICD-11 gates, honestly answered)

Tick all four, over 6+ months, and it is a clinical problem worth treating as CSBD:
1. Have I made numerous unsuccessful attempts to cut down or control it?
2. Has it become central enough that I neglect health, study/work, hygiene, or other responsibilities?
3. Have I continued despite real consequences (relationship, school/work, health)?
4. Do I keep doing it even when I get little or nothing out of it?
…plus marked or significant functional impairment. **Shame alone does not count.** High libido alone does not count. Common masturbation does not count.

## 6. What to do if it is genuinely a problem

**First line (WFSBP guidelines, Turner et al. 2022, *Dialogues Clin Neurosci* 24(1):10–69, PMID 37522807):** psychoeducation and psychotherapy, *always*. Zhu et al. 2025, *Front Psychiatry* 16:1621136 concurs: CBT/psychotherapy preferred, biopsychosocial, not a symptom to be silenced.

Evidence for specific modalities:
- **CBT for hypersexuality** — Hallberg, Kaldo & Arver 2019 RCT: reduced sexual compulsivity **and** reduced depressive symptoms vs control; male-only sample; group format, 7 weeks.
- **Mindfulness-based relapse prevention (MBRP)** — Holas et al. 2020, *J Behav Addict* 9(4):1088–1092, pilot, n=13: significantly less porn time plus reduced anxiety, depression, and OC symptoms over 8 weeks. Exploratory; needs RCTs.
- **ACT-based treatment** — 1-year follow-up (Frontiers in Psychology, 2026): hypersexuality reductions maintained at 12 months, 9/10 participants clinically improved, craving-interference reductions sustained. Uncontrolled, n=10.
- **Mindfulness generally** — systematic review (PMC11815347): some evidence for several sexual disorders, but CSBD studies too scarce to generalise.
- **Pharmacological:** no drug has a formal indication. SSRIs and **naltrexone** are the most relevant off-label options; few RCTs; choice depends on intensity and comorbidity. Naltrexone nuance: low acute doses *increase* orgasms, while chronic/higher doses diminish orgasm intensity, arousal, desire, and the orgasm-related prolactin rise (*Sexual Medicine Reviews*, 10.1093/sxmrev/qeaf023).

**What not to buy:** NoFap/Reboot superpowers · permanent testosterone boost · a 90-day reboot as a cure · "porn-induced ED" as a diagnosis · dopamine receptor damage · day-by-day flatline timelines · shame as a treatment (it is now the best-documented iatrogenic risk in this whole space).
