# Human Trafficking in Cambodia

## Summary

Cambodia is a major source, transit, and destination country for human trafficking, with the scam compound industry creating an unprecedented modern slavery crisis. At peak, 100,000–150,000 people were held in Cambodian compounds alone, recruited from 66+ countries under false job promises. Victims are confined in prison-like compounds, forced to run scams 12–16 hours/day, and subjected to torture, sexual violence, and ransom demands. The U.S. placed Cambodia on Tier 3 (lowest) for trafficking for the 4th straight year and designated it a "state sponsor" of trafficking for the first time in 2025. Cambodia never arrested or prosecuted a suspected scam compound operator or owner for years.

## Scale

- 100,000–150,000 people in Cambodian compounds at peak (UN OHCHR)
- Up to 300,000 region-wide (Cambodia + Myanmar)
- Victims recruited from 66+ countries
- Workers bought and sold between compounds for $40,000–$50,000 per person
- Workers coerced into recruiting friends ($40–$1,400 per person brought in)

## Types of Trafficking

| Type | Details |
|------|---------|
| Scam Compound Labor | Lured by fake job ads; passports confiscated; forced to scam 12–16 hrs/day |
| Sex Trafficking | Prevalent in casino/entertainment districts |
| Debt Bondage | Workers told they owe "recruitment fees" |
| Child Labor | Amnesty documented children as young as 14 inside compounds |

## Conditions Inside Compounds

- 3–4 meter concrete walls with barbed wire, armed guards (some with AK-47s)
- "Dark rooms" — designated torture chambers
- Beatings, electric shocks, forced exercise as punishment
- Sexual violence — at least 6 women raped by managers (Amnesty 2026)
- Ransom demanded from families with torture videos as leverage
- Reports of deaths from torture, untreated illness, and suicide
- One case involved a victim whose blood was repeatedly drained ("blood slave")

## Recruitment Pipeline

- Deceptive job ads on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Telegram ($2,000–$5,000/month salaries)
- Workers smuggled across borders through informal routes
- After Cambodia's 2019 online gambling ban, empty casinos repurposed into compounds
- Some trafficking victims coerced into recruiting friends

## Government Failure

- Never arrested or prosecuted a suspected scam compound operator/owner for years (U.S. State Dept 2025)
- None of Amnesty's 73 survivors in 2026 were officially recognized as trafficking victims
- Police documented meeting compound managers at gates and handing over specified individuals
- After 2026 crackdown, thousands of freed workers stranded on streets of Phnom Penh — no money, no passport, no shelter
- Women reported raped while sleeping under bridges
