# Face Investment: What Actually Pays

> **The third face page.** [[looksmaxxing-attractiveness-claims-audit]] checked the *claims*,
> [[non-facial-attractiveness-evidence]] checked the *body*, and this one answers the money
> question: **if you spend money on your face, what does it buy you?**
>
> Headline: **surgery is where people spend the most and get the least.** Skin, teeth, brows and
> stubble carry all the reliably measured effect and cost a small fraction of jaw surgery.

## Summary — the honest ranking

| Investment | Measured effect | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Skin (acne, PIH, scars) | Largest verified face effect | **Do it** |
| Facial hair (heavy stubble) | Ranked top of 4 beard levels | **Do it** — free |
| Teeth whitening / dental | Colour effect −0.56, largest dental lever | **Do it** |
| Eyebrows | Cheap, fast | **Do it** |
| Orthodontics | **+9%** (clinically weak), only if real malocclusion | **Conditional** |
| Orthognathic (jaw) surgery | **+5.5%** over ortho alone, only for real skeletal Class II | **Conditional — high risk if misused** |
| Chin augmentation | **+0.00 — null on every outcome** | **Don't** |
| Cosmetic facial surgery overall | **+0.08/10, P > .54 — null** | **Don't** |
| Buccal fat / jaw implants / v-taper surgery | No evidence found | **Don't** |
| Mewing, bone hacks | Adult bone change unsupported (AAO 2024) | **Don't** |

## The meta-analysis that settles orthodontics

**Kouskoura, Ochsner, Verna, Pandis & Kanavakis (2022)**, *Eur J Orthod*, doi 10.1093/ejo/cjac034.
PROSPERO-registered (CRD42020169904) systematic review and meta-analysis, 20 studies:
3 RCTs + 17 non-randomised. This is the highest-quality evidence available on the question.

- **Orthodontics vs untreated controls: +9.05% attractiveness** (95% CI 4.71-13.39).
- **Orthodontics + orthognathic surgery vs orthodontics alone: +5.51%** (95% CI 1.55-9.47).
- No difference between extraction and non-extraction approaches (MD −0.89, CI −8.72 to 6.94).
- No difference between Class II corrector types (MD 2.21, CI −16.51 to 20.93).
- **Quality is poor:** only 1 RCT at low risk of bias, 1 with some concerns, 1 at high risk; **all
  17 non-randomised studies were unclear or high risk of bias.**
- The authors' own conclusion: orthodontic treatment has a **"clinically weak effect"** on facial
  attractiveness.

**So: nine percent, with weak evidence, is the honest headline number for straightening your teeth
as an appearance investment.** It is worth doing if you have crowding or a bite problem on dental
health grounds, which is a separate and stronger reason.

## The jaw surgery finding you must read twice

**Tsang, McFadden, Wiltshire, Pershad & Baker (2009)**, *Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop*
135:66-72, doi 10.1016/j.ajodo.2007.01.033. 20 mandibular advancement patients, 20 laypeople +
20 orthodontists + 20 oral surgeons rating before/after profiles.

This is the most operationally useful paper on this page, because it says **when jaw surgery works
and when it backfires:**

- Profiles **consistently improved** when pretreatment profile angle was **≤157-159°** and
  ANB angle **≥5.5-6.5°**.
- **The incidence of a LESS desirable profile after surgery was 2.6-5.0× higher** when the
  pretreatment profile angle exceeded the threshold.
- Laypeople showed **no** trend between ANB angle and profile change — the specialists and the
  general public disagree about who benefits.

**Meaning:** mandibular advancement surgery helps people who actually have a skeletal Class II
(retruded jaw). On a face that already has a normal profile, the same procedure is a coin flip with
a documented downside.

**Ng, De Silva, Smit, De Silva & Farella (2012)**, *Eur J Orthod*, doi 10.1093/ejo/cjs028.
10 Class II patients, BSSO mandibular advancement, rated by 10 orthodontists + 10 art students +
10 laypeople:

- Lateral profile attractiveness: **+11.5%** (95% CI 9.4-13.5)
- Frontal: **+7.5%** (95% CI 5.4-9.5)
- **Ratings almost doubled when the pre/post status was disclosed** versus blinded evaluation.
  This is an expectancy effect and it is worth knowing, because most patients see before/after
  photos that are not blinded.

**Both of these study patients with a diagnosed skeletal malocclusion.** Neither supports jaw
surgery as a cosmetic upgrade for a normal-profile face.

## Why the surgery column is so bad

Already established in [[looksmaxxing-attractiveness-claims-audit]] and
[[non-facial-attractiveness-evidence]], repeated here because it is the money question:

- **Zimm et al. (2013)**, *JAMA Facial Plast Surg*, doi 10.1001/jamafacial.2013.268. 49 patients,
  50 blinded raters, ≥6-month follow-up. **+0.08/10, P > .54 — not significant.** Authors:
  surgery "did not consistently improve their attractiveness."
- **Parsa et al. (2019)**, *JAMA Facial Plast Surg*, doi 10.1001/jamafacial.2019.0463. 24 men,
  145 raters. Overall +0.29/7 [0.13, 0.46] — small. **Masculinity +0.13 [−0.03, 0.30], not
  significant. Chin augmentation: null on every outcome.**
- Neither study measures a partner, a marriage, or anyone's satisfaction. They are photo-rating
  proxies — which means the true real-world return is **unmeasured**, not just small.

## What actually has face-level evidence

### Skin — the strongest face lever
- Jones, Little, Burt & Perrett (2004), PMID 15250662: skin apparent health predicts perceived
  health, with a large effect, and drives attractiveness ratings.
- Nuance from the audit: skin is **not** stronger than masculinity as a predictor (Foo et al. 2017
  contradicted the older claim), but on a face that is already average in structure, **skin is
  where the remaining variance is.**
- Your skin is already your strongest feature — clear, no active acne, no visible scarring in the
  reference frames. The investment here is **maintenance, not correction**: daily SPF and
  retinoids. See [acne-treatment](../features/acne-treatment.md) and
  [skincare-basics](../features/skincare-basics.md).

### Facial hair — free, and the best-supported face change
See [hair-facial-hair-attractiveness](hair-facial-hair-attractiveness.md). Heavy stubble ranked
first out of four beard levels (Dixson et al. 2016, N = 8,520). Cost: a razor. This is the single
best face ROI available.

### Teeth — colour specifically
- Al Hamad et al. (2021), doi 10.1111/jerd.12834, n = 1,040: tooth **colour** was a **large** effect
  (−0.56), larger than diastema (−0.55) and far larger than any geometric dental parameter.
- **Skip the orthodontics-for-looks pitch** — 9%, weak evidence, thousands of dollars.
- **Do the cheap things**: whitening, and see [appearance-frauds](../features/appearance-frauds.md).

### Eyebrows
- Strong brows are already in your favour. Brow shaping is cheap and fast — see
  [eyebrow-shaping](../features/eyebrow-shaping.md).

## Age gate — the part that matters most here

**Orthognathic surgery is not an option until growth is complete.** Jaw and facial bones continue
changing through adolescence; operating before the growth pattern has stabilised risks operating on
a face that is still moving, and the outcome cannot be predicted. For males this conventionally
means **18+**, confirmed by a qualified orthodontist and maxillofacial surgeon — not by a
dermatologist or a barber.

Same for implants and permanent facial procedures. **If you are under 18, the entire surgical
column is closed to you** — which removes most of the temptation to waste money, and most of the
risk.

## If you have money to spend, in order

1. **Gym membership** — upper-body hypertrophy. r = .133, the only trait with mating *and*
   reproduction support. Better ROI than anything on this page.
2. **Skin routine** — sunscreen daily, retinoid, moisturiser. Maintain what already works.
3. **Teeth whitening** — largest measured dental effect.
4. **A proper haircut** — d = 0.67 territory (see the hair page).
5. **Dental consult** — if you have crowding or a bite issue, it gets fixed on health grounds and
   gets a 9% appearance bonus.
6. **Save the rest.** Surgical facial modification returns roughly nothing for men and carries
   real risk.
