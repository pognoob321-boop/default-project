# Political Repression and Human Rights Abuses in Cambodia

## Summary

Cambodia operates as a de facto single-party state under the Cambodian People's Party (CPP), which holds 100% of legislative seats since dissolving the main opposition CNRP in 2017. The government systematically suppresses political dissent, imprisons activists and journalists, crushes independent media, restricts civil society, and deploys Chinese-supplied surveillance infrastructure. As of mid-2026, 117 "prisoners of interest" are held including 53 political prisoners. The country ranks 161st out of 181 on the World Press Freedom Index. The EU, U.S., and UN have all condemned the crackdown, but international pressure has produced no meaningful improvement.

## Single-Party Rule

- CNRP dissolved November 2017 by Supreme Court ruling; 118 members banned from politics
- CPP won 100% of seats in 2018, 120/125 in 2023 (elections condemned as neither free nor fair)
- Kem Sokha (CNRP leader) sentenced to 27 years for "conspiracy with a foreign power"; appeal upheld April 2026
- Former PM Hun Sen remains Senate President and de facto power behind the throne

## Political Prisoners

As of mid-2026 (LICADHO):
- 117 total "prisoners of interest"
- 53 political prisoners
- 5 environmental activists
- 3 media workers
- 55 others (social media users, activists)

## Coerced Confessions

Government systematically coerces public apologies from detained activists:
- Confessions filmed in prison uniforms and published on Fresh News (government outlet)
- At least 90 such confessions published
- Failure to comply results in prolonged imprisonment
- Activists pressured to publicly apologize, renounce opposition, and join the ruling CPP

## Press Freedom

- Rank: 161 out of 181 countries (2025 World Press Freedom Index)
- The Cambodia Daily forced to close (2017) via bogus $6.3M tax bill
- Voice of Democracy license revoked (2023)
- Two journalists sentenced to 14 years for treason (Feb 2026) — first in Cambodian history
- 65 websites blocked including RFA, Cambodia Daily, VOD
- Five media licenses revoked in 2025
- Six journalists imprisoned in 2025

## Civil Society Crackdowns

- **LANGO Law (2015)**: Mandatory registration; vague "security" grounds for denial
- National Democratic Institute ordered closed (2017)
- Equitable Cambodia (land rights NGO) suspended; director charged with incitement (2024)
- EU Parliament resolution (492 votes, Nov 2024) condemned harassment of civil society
- 2024 UPR: 48 recommendations on civic space; 18 refused by Cambodia

## Union Suppression

- Trade Union Law (2016): Burdensome registration requirements
- 34+ local unions stuck in registration limbo for up to 4+ years
- One union at Poipet factory submitted 11 applications over 2 years
- EU partially suspended EBA benefits in 2020 partly over freedom of association

## Surveillance State

- 487 AI-equipped CCTV cameras with facial recognition across Phnom Penh (2025)
- National Internet Gateway planned — all traffic through government-controlled infrastructure
- China supplied biometric surveillance, DNA screening equipment (2021)
- 2015 Telecommunications Law permits undeclared monitoring of "any private speech"
- No comprehensive data protection law
- Only 47% of respondents felt free to speak on social media (2021 survey)

## CLV-DTA Crackdown (2024)

- 94+ arrested (including children) for criticizing the Cambodia-Laos-Vietnam Development Triangle
- 59+ detained by year's end despite government's September withdrawal from CLV-DTA
- Most charges not dropped after withdrawal

## Transnational Repression

- Six CNRP supporters deported from Thailand and charged with treason (Nov 2024), including a 7-year-old
- Lim Kimya (exiled opposition) shot dead in Bangkok (Jan 2025) — widely viewed as political assassination
- Thailand repeatedly arrested and returned Cambodian dissidents

## 2025 Citizenship Revocation Law

- Constitution amended (July 2025) to allow stripping citizenship for "treason" or "colluding with foreign powers"
- Over 50 CSOs warned of statelessness risk
