# ADHD Screening & Diagnosis: What the Evidence Actually Supports

Companion to [[study-quit-loop-and-beginner-method]]. Answers the third question: *"Is my focus problem a study technique issue, an environment issue, or a real attention disorder — how do I tell them apart, and what's the highest-leverage fix?"*

**Read this line before anything else: a positive ADHD screen in a general-population young person is wrong ~9 times out of 10.** The base rate is low and self-report screens over-fire badly. This page exists to prevent a teenager concluding he is broken, and to prevent a parent spending scarce money on a diagnosis that the literature says is unlikely to pay off academically anyway.

---

## 1. Start with the base rate, not the symptom

| Population | Prevalence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Adults, persistent childhood-onset | **2.58%** (1.51–4.45) | Song et al. 2021, 40 studies / 30 countries, PMID [33692893](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33692893/) |
| Adults, symptomatic (any onset) | **6.76%** (4.31–10.61) | same |
| Children/adolescents, all ages | **8.0%** (6.0–10) | 2023 umbrella review, 13 meta-analyses, 588 studies, **3,277,590 participants** |
| **Ages 12–18 specifically** | **5.6%** | BMC Psychiatry 2023 global meta-analysis |

**Roughly 1 in 20 adolescents, not 1 in 3.** Underdiagnosis in girls is real and well-documented (girls meet criteria at just under half boys' rates; Hinshaw et al. 2021, PMID [34231220](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34231220/); girls diagnosed ~5 years later) — but underdiagnosis does not move the base rate to the point where a self-test result means much on its own.

## 2. The screener will mislead you — this is the single most important finding

**Chamberlain, Cortese & Grant 2021, *Compr Psychiatry* 106:152224, PMID 33581449, PMC7116749 — verified first-hand against the full text.**

> "The tool resulted in considerable overestimation of ADHD, indicating probable ADHD in **26.0%** and **17.3%** of participants, as compared to expected prevalence of **2.5%**. The estimated **positive predictive value was only ~11.5%**… this indicates that using the ASRS in general population samples will result in **7–10 times over-identification** of ADHD."

Two non-treatment-seeking cohorts (UK N=642, USA N=579), both with **normal trait impulsivity** on the Barratt Impulsiveness Scale. Their stated mechanism for false positives: **impulse-control disorders and obsessive-compulsive disorders, which the ASRS captures but does not distinguish** — "these are very rarely screened for."

**Their 4-point pre-referral gate** (use it verbatim):

1. collateral support for symptoms (from a partner or family member)
2. symptoms were evident in childhood
3. symptoms occur in **two or more functional domains**
4. no other obvious disorder better accounts for the problems

### Sensitivity/specificity varies enormously with base rate

| Study | Sens. | Spec. | PPV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kessler 2005 (original, N=154) | 68.7% | 99.5% | — |
| Ustun 2017 ASRS-5, general population **at 8.2% prevalence** | 91.4% | 96.0% | **67.3%** |
| Ustun 2017, clinical sample at 57.7% prevalence | 91.9% | 74.0% | 82.8% |
| **Chamberlain 2021, general population at 2.5%** | — | — | **~11.5%** |
| Brevik 2020 | — | **0.45 at sens 0.95** | — |
| ASRS-5 German primary care 2022 | 95.6% | 72.3% | — |

The 67–83% PPV figures are real **only when prevalence is already ~8%**. In an actual general-population young-adult sample, PPV collapses to ~11.5%.

### And the ASRS is not validated for adolescents

NIDA Data Share instrument documentation, verbatim: the ASRS-V1.1 Screener "while **not formally validated in adolescents**… **there is no validated, accepted standard self report screening assessment for ADHD in adolescents.**" ([datashare.nida.nih.gov](https://datashare.nida.nih.gov/instrument/adult-adhd-self-report-rating-scale))

**Do not hand a 16-year-old the ASRS and treat the result as meaningful.** The instruments named in NICE NG87 for this age group are the **Conners' rating scales** and the **Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire (SDQ)** — both with parent, teacher and self-report versions.

## 3. How to tell it apart from a phone problem and a motivation problem

### The discriminator that matters: does it follow you across settings with different incentives?

DSM-5 requires impairment in **more than one major setting**; AAP 2019 KAS 2 makes this a **Grade B strong recommendation**, with information obtained from parents/guardians, school personnel, and the child. Thresholds: **≥6 symptoms inattention and/or ≥6 hyperactivity-impulsivity for ≤16 y** (≥5 each for 17+), ≥6 months, **onset before age 12**.

**The asymmetry that separates them:** preferring your phone over a textbook is a *within-one-setting* phenomenon. ADHD requires the failure to show up **when motivation is high and the stimulus is novel** — in a game, a sport, a conversation, a job they actually want. If the failure only appears in front of material you find aversive, the DSM criterion for cross-situational impairment is not met.

This is DSM-derived reasoning, **not** a validated instrument. No published study validates a "phone-free motivated task" test — do not claim otherwise.

### The objective test is cross-informant agreement

Parent–teacher agreement on ADHD is **low**, which cuts both ways:

| Source | Statistic |
|---|---|
| Fu et al. meta-analysis (11 studies) | pooled Cohen's **κ = 0.25** (0.18–0.31), I²=75.8%; **clinical samples κ = 0.07** |
| IMAGE study, N=1383 probands | item κ = 0.01–0.15; dimensional **r = 0.12–0.16** |
| Developmentally | Inattention **r = 0.18–0.32**; Hyperactivity/Impulsivity **r = 0.33–0.44** |
| Longitudinal cohort, N=8329 | r = **0.107** inattention, 0.241 hyperactivity, 0.093 combined at age 12 |

**Teaching point:** ADHD is easy to *over*-detect from one informant. If a parent and a teacher independently report the same thing in different settings, that is genuinely informative. If only the teenager says it, that is the weakest possible signal.

### Family history — a prompt to ask, not a verdict

Faraone & Larsson: mean heritability **74%** across 37 twin studies; siblings of probands have ~**9×** increased risk. **But** heritability is a population statistic for symptom variance. It does **not** mean "80% of people with ADHD have an ADHD parent," and it does not make family history a diagnostic test. NICE's own framing is the usable one: "The diagnosis of ADHD in older family members such as parents may have previously been missed and **should be considered**."

### ADHD vs problematic phone use — no validated discriminator exists

Meta-analysis, 14 studies, **2,488 participants** ages 6–18: ADHD groups showed significantly more severe problematic internet use than non-clinical controls, **but NOT versus clinical controls**. The authors state plainly: *"the small number of studies does not allow for a systematic comparison between ADHD and groups with other psychopathologies"* ([Werling et al. 2022, *J Behav Addict* 11:305–25, PMID 35567763](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35567763/)).

Causation is **bidirectional** — Thorell et al. 2023 (*JCPP*, PMC11272698): "reciprocal associations… more consistent for **problematic use** than for screen time."

What *is* documented and separates them: problematic smartphone use carries **tolerance, withdrawal symptoms when the phone is unavailable, and functional impairment**. Withdrawal-on-separation and tolerance are **not** DSM ADHD criteria. Problematic smartphone use affects **~1 in 4** young people and is associated with poorer sleep ([Sohn et al. 2019, *BMC Psychiatry* 19:356, PMID 31779637](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31779637/)).

### Sleep is the mimic with the strongest evidence

Sleep deprivation **produces deficits resembling or exacerbating ADHD symptoms**; hyperactivity-impulsivity risk was **3.2× greater** with shorter sleep duration at 41 months (PMC3630973). Children with ADHD have significantly higher **bedtime resistance (z = 6.94, p<.001)** ([Cortese et al. 2009, PMID 19625983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19625983/)). Bidirectional: stimulants delay sleep onset, poor sleep worsens symptoms.

**Fix sleep before anything else.** It costs nothing and it is the one mimic with a free intervention.

### "I'm just not disciplined" vs executive-function deficits

Boonstra, Oosterlaan, Sergeant & Buitelaar 2005 (13 studies): **inhibition d = 0.64–0.89** — the largest effect. Inhibition is *stopping what you already started*, which is exactly the reported complaint. But **word reading d = 0.60 and colour naming d = 0.62** — the deficits are **not specific**. The authors themselves call for better tests of specificity. **Note: this paper is 21 years old; no superseding meta-analysis was found.** Do not present it as current, and do not present it as diagnostic.

## 4. Medication: the academic-outcome finding, verified

The belief "medication improves short-term symptoms but produces no durable academic gains" is **correct**, and the evidence is stronger than that — the finding is that no treatment arm produced a lasting group difference at all.

| Timepoint | Finding |
|---|---|
| **MTA 1999**, N=579, ages 7–9, 14-month RCT | MedMgt > Behavioural on **ADHD symptoms**. But on academic achievement, **only Combined treatment beat community care** — **MedMgt alone did not.** NIMH's own summary: "combination treatment was consistently superior to routine community care, whereas **medication alone or behavioral treatment alone were not**." |
| **24 months** | effect size **half** of post-treatment; ~36% approached normalisation |
| **36 months** (Jensen 2007, PMID 17667478) | **"treatment groups did not differ significantly on any measure"** |
| **8 years** (Molina 2010, PMC3063150) | **"sustained absence of long-term effects of an initial period of randomly assigned treatment"**; medication use fell 62%; participants **worse than the local normative comparison group on 91% of variables tested** |

**NIMH's own programme headline:** *"Short-term Intensive Treatment Not Likely to Improve Long-term Outcomes for Children with ADHD (March 2009)."*

**Counterweight, stated for honesty:** a 36-month propensity-score/growth-mixture re-analysis found gains "maintained… even after this component of treatment was discontinued." The authors themselves call this "a controversial topic." Both are legitimate; the mainstream finding is the null.

**Cortese et al. 2018** (*Lancet Psychiatry* 5:727–38, PMID 30097390) — **this paper reports no academic-performance outcome at all.** Any figure claiming it does is fabricated. Its actual numbers: clinician-rated child efficacy — amphetamines SMD −1.02, methylphenidate −0.78, atomoxetine −0.56. **Teacher-rated**, only methylphenidate (−0.82) and modafinil (−0.76) beat placebo; **no teacher data existed for amphetamines**. The headline "amphetamines win" is a clinician-in-the-room result that does not replicate in a classroom. The paper's own line: **"We did not find sufficient data for the 26-week and 52-week timepoints."**

Side effects, for the parent conversation: weight SMD −0.71 to −0.93 in children; BP rises; growth suppression ~**1 cm/yr for the first 14 months, absent by year 3, no rebound** ([Swanson 2007](https://dev-hinshaw-lab.pantheon.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/2007_1-s2.0-S0890856709615525-main.pdf)); **16-year data**: consistent stimulant treatment associated with **reduced adult height, increased weight and BMI** ([Greenhill 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0890856719314431)).

## 5. What actually helps — and it's free

### Organisational skills training — the best academic evidence in the entire literature

12 RCTs, **1,054 children**, PROSPERO CRD42015019261 ([Bikic et al. 2017, *Clin Psychol Rev* 52:108–23, PMID 28088557](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28088557/)):

- Organisational skills: **g = 0.54** teacher-rated, **g = 0.83** parent-rated
- Inattention: g = 0.26 teacher, g = 0.56 parent
- **Academic performance: g = 0.33 (0.14–0.51)**
- **GPA: g = 0.29 (0.07–0.51)**

Small, but it is **the only thing in this entire review that reliably touches academic outcomes without medication** — and it directly targets "I drop off for two days." It is a checklist and a planner. **No diagnosis required.**

### Exercise

Umbrella review of 45 meta-analyses: core symptoms **SMD 0.50 (0.39–0.61)**, executive function **0.77 (0.62–0.93)** — but **no significant effect on sleep quality, BMI, or academic achievement.** Certainty **low**. ([Zhu et al., *Psychiatry Res* 364:117267, PMID 42284935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42284935/)). Best-supported dose: low-intensity aerobic, **70 min, 2×/week, ≥12 weeks**.

**Honest framing:** exercise is worth doing for symptoms and for being well. It is not a study intervention and will not move grades.

### Educational accommodations — free, and don't need a diagnosis

The structural point is the useful one: *"accommodations adjust the delivery of the academic program to the student's current state, **changing the expectations for the student**"* — unlike interventions, which aim to change the student's skills ([JAACAP 2021 systematic review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2020.07.891)). Extended time, reduced copying demands, a fixed seat away from the door. **Highest-leverage thing a parent can request without a diagnosis.**

### Do not spend money on Cogmed

Near-transfer yes, **far-transfer no.** "No convincing evidence of the generalization of working memory training to other skills" ([Melby-Lervåg & Hulme 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028228)); 87 publications, "no evidential value… using treated controls" ([Melby-Lervåg, Redick & Hulme 2016](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1745691616635612)). Chacko et al.: Cogmed "should not be used as a treatment for ADHD in children." One meta-analysis reporting SMD −0.47 on inattention is a **contested outlier**.

### Parent training — works, but not for grades

29 studies, 138 effect sizes, N=2,345: small-to-medium effects on parental outcomes; the active components are **antecedent manipulation** and **reinforcement**, and **higher psychoeducation dosage was *negatively* related to outcomes** ([Dekkers et al. 2022, PMID 34224837](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34224837/)). Cochrane: *"No study reported data for academic achievement."* Blinded-rater analyses collapse the symptom effects. **NICE allows parent training before diagnosis** (NG87 §1.1.1.3).

## 6. Referral pathway — and an honest Cambodia problem

**NICE NG87 §1.3.2, the rule:** *"A diagnosis of ADHD should not be made solely on the basis of rating scale or observational data."* §1.2.9: primary care should **not** make the initial diagnosis in children. §1.2.8: refer to secondary care if problems persist with **at least moderate impairment**.

### The access problem is documented, and it is bad

- **WHO: 75% in many low-income countries do not have access to the treatment they need.**
- **ADHD is not on the WHO mhGAP priority list.** The priority conditions are depression, schizophrenia/psychosis, suicide, epilepsy, dementia, alcohol use disorders, illicit drug use, and mental disorders in children. This is a hard fact about why formal ADHD pathways are unlikely to exist at Cambodian primary-care level.
- UNICEF 2022: **70%** of people aged 15+ with mental health conditions lack adequate care; the gap is higher in low- and middle-income countries.
- Counter-framing worth knowing: across World Mental Health Surveys (24 countries, 63,678 participants), **lack of perceived need for treatment was by far the most frequently reported reason** for not seeking care. The treatment gap is partly a demand-side phenomenon.

> **Gap: no Cambodia-specific ADHD service data, clinician counts, or wait times were found. Anything specific about Cambodian services is currently unsourced.** Do not assert it.

### The lowest-cost viable route, in order

1. **Ask a teacher, not a doctor.** Because cross-informant agreement is the objective test (κ ≈ 0.25), the highest-value missing input is a **teacher-rated Conners or SDQ in Khmer**. It costs nothing, it can be translated, and requesting it is not requesting a diagnosis.
2. **Get Conners + SDQ (parent, teacher, self) before seeking any clinician.** These are the two instruments NICE names.
3. **Screen and fix sleep first.** Free, strongest mimic evidence, fix is a consistent wake time.
4. **Use NICE's pre-diagnosis allowance:** group-based ADHD-focused parent support "should not wait for a formal diagnosis," after watchful waiting up to 10 weeks. Even if no such group exists locally, the framing holds — **you do not need a diagnosis to start.**
5. **Request accommodations now.** Free, and explicitly allowed alongside or after interventions.
6. **Start organisational skills training.** Best academic evidence, no diagnosis required.
7. **Get moving.** SMD 0.50 on symptoms, free.

## 7. Do not cite these

- The "Kessler et al. 2007 cross-cultural validation paper" — **searched three ways, does not exist as described.** The 2007 Kessler paper is a US health-plan validation (PMID 17623385). The cross-cultural ASRS work is 2024 and 42-country.
- **Any Cortese 2018 academic-performance or quality-of-life figure.** Cortese 2018 has neither. QoL numbers live in a separate 2024 paper (PMID 38823477).
- Any named hearing/vision/thyroid/trauma rule-out attributed to NICE NG87 or AAP 2019 — those guidelines say only "rule out other conditions" generically. Not verified.
- "Cohen d range 0.51–5.15" from PMID 27163988 — almost certainly a typo.
- Any "phone addiction vs ADHD test." It does not exist.
- PMC12229068 as the exercise umbrella review — PMCID unconfirmed; identified the paper by content (Zhu et al., PMID 42284935).

## Methodology

Delegated research pass over PubMed/PMC, PsycNet, NICE, AAP, WHO and UNICEF, followed by **first-hand verification of the single load-bearing claim** (Chamberlain 2021 PPV) by reading the full PMC text. Sub-questions: (1) screener validity and base rate, (2) prevalence by age band, (3) ADHD vs motivation vs phone-use discriminators, (4) medication and academic outcomes, (5) non-drug interventions with academic endpoints, (6) the referral pathway including low-resource access.
