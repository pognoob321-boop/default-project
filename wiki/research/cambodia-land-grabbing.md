# Land Grabbing and Land Rights Violations in Cambodia

## Summary

Cambodia has granted over 2.2 million hectares as Economic Land Concessions (ELCs) to private companies, displacing an estimated 777,000 Cambodians. ELCs directly account for up to 40% of Cambodia's total deforestation, with 773,000 hectares of forest lost within ELC areas between 2001 and 2022. The concession system is deeply intertwined with the political elite — tycoon-senators hold vast land holdings, military forces enforce seizures, and activists face imprisonment or assassination. Only 43 of 458 indigenous communities (9.4%) received Collective Land Titles between 2011 and 2024.

## Scale

- 2.2 million hectares granted as ELCs (LICADHO, Sep 2025)
- 290–330 ELC projects granted; 33 revoked
- 777,000 Cambodians affected by land conflicts and evictions (ADHOC)
- 133,000 Phnom Penh residents (~10% of city) evicted since 1990
- 34 ongoing land conflict cases in 2025 impacting 8,559 households across 10,852 hectares

## Key Features of ELCs

- Long-term leases (originally 70–99 years, reduced to 50 years in 2015)
- 2001 Land Law caps ELCs at 10,000 hectares — numerous concessions far exceed this
- Moratorium declared May 2012 but poorly enforced
- Despite covering 2.2M+ hectares, government expected only $4M in tax revenue from ELCs in 2025
- Many ELCs lie dormant after forests were cleared, with no economic benefits realized

## Notable Cases

### Dara Sakor / Union Development Group (UDG)
- 36,000 hectares for 99 years (3.6x the legal limit)
- Covers ~20% of Cambodia's coastline in Koh Kong province
- $3.8 billion project: luxury resort, casino, deep-water port, airport
- Over 1,000 families displaced; military used to burn houses
- Parts within Botum Sakor National Park (endangered species habitat)
- U.S. Treasury sanctioned in 2021 for "seizure and demolition of locals' land"

### Boeung Kak Lake
- 99-year lease for $79 million covering 133 hectares
- 20,000 people faced displacement; 4,000+ families affected
- Developer began filling the lake with sand, worsening flooding
- World Bank froze $70 million in annual loans over the evictions
- "Boeung Kak 13" — 13 women activists sentenced to 2.5 years for protesting
- "The largest relocation of people in Cambodia since the Khmer Rouge emptied the cities"

### Sugar Concessions (Koh Kong)
- 12 land activists convicted of incitement in 2024 for petitioning over decade-long land disputes involving tycoons Ly Yong Phat and Heng Huy

## Violence Against Activists

Cambodia is one of the most dangerous places for environmental rights defenders:

- **Mother Nature Cambodia**: 5 activists imprisoned 700+ days; 5–8 year sentences; 70+ NGOs demanding release; appeals hearing postponed indefinitely (June 2026)
- **Ouch Leng** (Goldman Prize winner): Detained by military investigating illegal logging; forced to flee country
- **Lim Kimya** (exiled opposition lawmaker): Shot dead in Bangkok (Jan 2025)
- **Chhoeung Chheng**: Journalist shot and killed reporting on illegal logging (Dec 2024)
- **102 people arrested/imprisoned** in 2024 for activism (LICADHO)

## Deforestation Impact

- Cambodia lost 2.92 million hectares of tree cover (2001–2024) — 33% of tree cover at millennium's start
- ELCs directly account for up to 40% of total deforestation
- 773,000 hectares of forest lost within ELC areas (2001–2022)
- 56% of Cambodia's 2024 tree cover loss occurred within protected areas
- Botum Sakor National Park: 32,000 hectares of primary forest lost (twice the size of Singapore)

## Indigenous Communities

- 458 indigenous communities across 15 provinces (~1.4% of population)
- Only 43 of 458 (9.4%) received Collective Land Titles (2011–2024)
- Existing CLTs cover only 41,538 hectares — fraction of 4M hectares traditionally managed
- 2023 Environment Code replaced "indigenous" with "local" communities — a major setback
