# Hair & Facial Hair Attractiveness Evidence

> Companion to [looksmaxxing-attractiveness-claims-audit](looksmaxxing-attractiveness-claims-audit.md)
> (the face's geometry) and [non-facial-attractiveness-evidence](non-facial-attractiveness-evidence.md)
> (everything else). This page covers **scalp hair, hair loss, hairstyle quality, and facial hair**.
>
> The wiki previously had beard advice with **zero citations** (`features/coloring-style.md`).
> This page is where that advice either gets evidence or gets deleted.

## Summary

Three findings that will change what you do this week:

1. **Heavy stubble beats everything else, and clean-shaven is last.** Dixson et al. 2016 (N = 8,520
   women): heavy stubble most attractive, then light stubble, then full beards, **clean-shaven least
   attractive of all**. Boothroyd et al. 2013 independently found the same ordering. The wiki's
   "match the beard to your face shape" advice has no evidence behind it.
2. **Facial hair hides bad face shape.** Dixson et al. 2016: stubble and beards **dampen** the
   polarizing effect of extreme masculinity or femininity on attractiveness ratings. This is the
   strongest argument for wearing a beard, and it is not about jawlines.
3. **Beard fashion is a conformity penalty.** Janif, Brooks & Dixson 2014: beards and stubble were
   rated *more* attractive when they were **rare** in the stimulus set. Being the same as everyone
   around you costs you.

**Biggest evidence gap: nearly all hairstyle research is on women.** The shine/alignment/volume
work, the hair-length work, the haircut-matching work — almost all female samples. There is
effectively **no** male hairstyle-attractiveness literature.

## Facial hair (beard / stubble)

**Verdict: SUPPORTED, with a clear peak at heavy stubble and a conformity caveat.**

| Rank | Style | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Heavy stubble** | Dixson 2016 (N=8,520), Boothroyd 2013, Janif 2014, Neave & Shields 2008 |
| 2 | Light stubble | Dixson 2016 second; Boothroyd 2013 rated it similar to clean-shaven |
| 3 | Full beard | Dixson 2016 third; but **first** when judged for long-term, parenting ability, health |
| 4 | Clean-shaven | Dixson 2016 last; Boothroyd 2013 similar to light stubble |

- **Dixson, Sulikowski, Gouda-Vossos, Rantala & Brooks (2016)**, *J Evol Biol* 29:2311-2320
  (doi 10.1111/jeb.12958), **N = 8,520 women**. Computer-morphed faces at −50%, −25%, 0, +25%,
  +50% masculinity, crossed with four beard levels and three relationship contexts.
  Main effect of facial hair: F(3, 1277) = 58.60, p < .001. **Heavy stubble > light stubble > full
  beard > clean-shaven (last).**
  - **The masking result is the useful one.** Facial hair × masculinity interaction:
    F(12, 1277) = 8.28, p < .001. Extremely masculinised *and especially* extremely feminised faces
    were least attractive **when clean-shaven**, and stubble/beard attenuated both.
  - Relationship context: facial hair × context F(6, 1277) = 17.33, p < .001. Beards and stubble beat
    clean-shaven **for long-term** relationships; stubble beat full beard for short-term.
- **Boothroyd et al. (2013)**, "The role of facial hair in women's perceptions of men's
  attractiveness, health, masculinity and parenting abilities", S1090513813000226. Real
  photographs of the same men at four beard levels (not fake beards, not make-up — this is why it
  is the good study). Women judged **heavy stubble most attractive**. Men rated full beards and
  heavy stubble most attractive, clean-shaven and light stubble least.
- **Masculinity is linear, attractiveness is not.** Masculinity ratings rose monotonically with
  facial hair, but attractiveness, health and parenting ratings were **non-linear**. So "more beard
  = more masculine" is true and useless for attraction — you are optimising the wrong curve.
- **Full beard wins on the long-term and fathering ratings.** Both sexes rated full beards highest
  for parenting ability and healthiness. If the goal is a partner rather than a date, full beard is
  a defensible choice.
- **Conformity penalty — negative frequency-dependent selection.** Janif, Brooks & Dixson (2014),
  *Biology Letters*, doi 10.1098/rsbl.2013.0958, PMC4013690. Facial hair main effect
  F(2.5, 4181) = 36.62, p < .001; **facial hair × treatment F(5, 4181) = 11.23, p < .001**.
  Beards and stubble were rated *more* attractive when beards were rare (full beard vs rare-beard
  treatment: t = 5.46, p < .001) and *less* attractive when they were common. Clean-shaven was
  least attractive in the even treatment. **Practical read: a beard style gains value when it is not
  what everyone around you has.** This is novelty preference, and it means the "right" beard
  changes with local fashion.
- **Meta-analytic status:** Mendo Miguez Barroso (2019), "After all, what are women attracted to?"
  reviewed facial hair among eight male traits and concluded it is **a relevant factor in
  attractiveness that needs future research** — not settled, but real. In the same review body hair
  was **less relevant** than other traits. Practical: scalp hair matters, body hair largely does not.

### What this kills

- **"Match the beard to your face shape."** No study supports jawline-conditional beard choice.
  The wiki's `features/coloring-style.md` claims (long face → avoid long beards; weak jawline →
  fuller beard to mask structure) are aesthetic folklore. There is one real study adjacent to it —
  Dixson 2016's masking result — and it says facial hair helps regardless of which structure is weak,
  because it attenuates *shape* extremity in either direction.
- **"Clean-shaven is a strong look."** It has never come first in a study using graded beard levels.

## Hair loss (male pattern baldness / androgenetic alopecia)

**Verdict: SUPPORTED — a real, fairly large physical-attractiveness penalty, and a public-health
grade of one.**

- **Henss (2024)**, "Toupee or not Toupee? Sic (et Non). Social perceptions of male pattern
  baldness", doi 10.32388/uykbf4. **Within-subject design**: 13 men with full AGA photographed
  once bald and once wearing a high-quality custom toupee — same man, same lighting, same day.
  - **d = 0.67 for attractiveness** (+0.71 points on a 9-point scale, 3.10 vs 2.39,
    p < .001). That is a large effect.
  - **Women were harsher than men**, d = 0.58 (2.65 vs 3.14). Women rated *every one* of the 13 men
    more negatively than men did, in both hair conditions (11/13 significant, d 0.31-0.73).
  - **Health effect was trivial: d = 0.14.**
  - **Self-assurance: no effect of hair condition at all.** Neither group-level nor individual-level.
    This is the finding people get wrong — restoring hair does not restore perceived confidence.
  - The gain was **uneven**: two men gained enormously, two gained little, and in rare cases a
    toupee was rated *worse*. There is no way to predict which from the face.
- **Kranz, Nadarevic & Erdfelder (2019)**, "Bald and bad? Experimental evidence for a dual-process
  account of baldness stereotyping", PMC7037739, 3 experiments.
  - **Physical attractiveness penalty is huge and survives everything**: F(1, 104) = 57.58,
    p < .001, **η²p = .36**; paired comparison d = 1.74. Present with *and* without
    individuating information.
  - **The social penalty reverses once people know you.** Facial-hair... hair condition ×
    individuating information F(1, 104) = 14.27, p < .001, η²p = .12. With portraits only, bald
    targets were less socially attractive (F = 9.19, p < .01). **With a character description
  attached, bald targets became *more* socially attractive** (F = 5.25, p = .02). The authors call
    this the "bald but nice" finding.
  - Mechanism: the attractiveness stereotype activates *implicitly* regardless, but is
    **suppressed at the explicit level by individuating information**. Both effects held even when
    the descriptions contained counterbalanced negative content.
- **Cash (1990)**, "Losing hair, losing points?", *J Soc Behav Pers* (doi
  10.1111/j.1559-1816.1990.tb00404.x). 18 matched slide pairs, 54 men + 54 women. MPB produced
  less favourable first impressions across the board — lower physical attractiveness, judged less
  desirable personal characteristics, and **age misperception**. Moderators (rater sex, rater age,
  target age) mostly did nothing. **The baldness stereotype was substantially attenuated once
  physical attractiveness was statistically controlled** — i.e. most of the penalty is the
  attractiveness stereotype itself, wearing a costume.
- **Korean data** (closest available to Southeast Asian social norms): Lee et al. 2002,
  PMID 12492971, 130 women + 90 non-balding men + 30 balding men. **Over 90%** perceived balding men
  as older and less attractive. Women reported "less attractive" significantly more often than
  non-balding men (p < .05). Balding men themselves reported "less confident" more often than
  non-balding men (p < .05) — i.e. self-perception tracks other-perception.
- **Dissenting result:** "Impression formation as a function of male baldness" (1998, 96
  undergraduates, computer-morphed full cranial hair onto a naturally bald 30-year-old). Full-hair
  was rated significantly more **dominant, dynamic, masculine and younger** — but there was
  **no difference in attractiveness**. One study, older, but a real counterweight.

**Actionable:** treat hair as a genuine appearance lever, not a vanity one — d = 0.67 to 1.74 is
larger than almost anything else in this entire wiki. Treatment options and safety are on
[hair-growth](../features/hair-growth.md). The key expectation to set correctly: **hair affects how
attractive you are judged, not how healthy or how self-assured you are judged.**

## Hairstyle quality (shine, alignment, volume, colour)

**Verdict: SUPPORTED — but the evidence is female, and the finding is counter-intuitive.**

- **Will, Beckmann, Kunstmann, Kerschbaumer & Loh (2025)**, PMC13068049, PMID 41116716.
  Experiment 1: n = 500 each from **Germany, Spain and the USA**. Experiment 2: **n = 2,000** US
  women, professional stylists manipulating natural wigs, three head orientations, blonde and brown.
  - **Shine wins:** high-shine hair rated more youthful, healthier and more attractive across all
    three countries.
  - **Alignment wins bigger:** **straight-aligned hair** rated most youthful, healthy and attractive
    regardless of colour or orientation. Shine mattered, but less than alignment.
  - **Volume is not a positive.** High volume *hurt* youthfulness and attractiveness in the front
    view, and only worked in combination with alignment and shine. Best-rated combination was
    **straight-aligned + high shine + LOW volume**.
  - **Colour barely mattered.** Little difference between neutral blonde and dark brown.
    Effect sizes: hair feature × orientation η²p = .19; the colour interactions were η²p = .05-.07
    and the authors question their ecological validity.
- **"Visual attractiveness is leaky" (2015)**, PMC4390982. Two findings that matter for how you get
  judged:
  - Face attractiveness **leaks** into hair ratings regardless of task order; hair leaks into face
    ratings too. **You cannot be assessed on your hair in isolation from your face.**
  - Both faces (d = 0.63) and hairstyles (d = 0.79) were rated **higher in isolation** than in
    face+hair composites. Incongruent combinations read as fake. So face-hair congruence matters
    more than either element's individual quality.

**Caveat you must not skip: every one of these studies used female targets.** There is no
equivalent male-hair study. The transferable principle is *shine and neat alignment read as
healthy and youthful* — that is probably not male-specific — but the volume finding and the
colour finding should not be assumed to transfer.

## Hair length

**Verdict: NO EVIDENCE for men.**

- The multiple-fitness-model work (Bereczkei & Meskó 2006) is **female targets, male raters**:
  long hair on women rated attractive regardless of face quality, and long hair was attributed
  determined/intelligent/independent/healthy traits versus short hair honest/caring/feminine.
- Nothing equivalent exists for men's hair length. Anyone telling you a specific men's haircut
  length is attractive is citing barbershop folklore, not research.

## Evidence gaps

1. **Male hairstyle attractiveness is essentially unstudied.** Same gap as male gait.
2. **No study tests whether a given beard or haircut suits a given face.** The entire
   "match your face shape" industry rests on aesthetic assertion.
3. **No study measures long-term mate outcomes for hair restoration.** Every hair-loss finding above
   is a first-impression rating study.
4. **Southeast Asian samples are absent again.** The Korean study is the nearest regional data point
   and it is 2002 with n = 250.
