# Train Your Brain to Crave Doing Hard Things (Dr. Izzy Sealey)

## Summary

Cambridge-trained doctor Dr. Izzy Sealey argues modern life is "friction-starved" (Hadza hunter-gatherers ~16,000 steps/day vs ~4,000 for us; thinking outsourced to AI/tools), so the brain never gets the hormetic stress and neuroplastic challenge it needs to grow. Willpower-based behavior change fails because identity doesn't change; the fix is to re-cast yourself as "someone built for friction" and deliberately engineer 2–4 kinds of worthwhile friction back into the week as small experiments.

## Takeaways

**Mechanism (why hard feels hard)**
- Hormesis (Mark Mattson): small repeated stressors strengthen systems.
- Neuroplasticity (Eleanor Maguire taxi-driver study): brains reorganize under challenge — use it or lose it.
- Hebb's law: neurons that fire together wire together; avoidance wires avoidance.
- Core loop to break: hard thing → discomfort → escape → relief. Replace with: hard thing → discomfort → pause → continue.

**4 friction categories — pick 1–2 each, run as 7-day experiments**
1. Physical: stairs over lift, walk trips <1 km, ~30 s cold at end of shower, sauna, lift something heavy 2–3×/week, time-restricted eating.
2. Sensory (boredom/silence — lets the default mode network consolidate): 5 min sitting still with no phone, one meal screen-free, walk/drive with no podcast, no phone first 20–30 min after waking, one screen-free evening/week.
3. Cognitive (anti-AI-atrophy): think/write your answer *before* opening AI, then refine; read primary sources not summaries; mental math before calculator; route in your head before Google Maps; handwrite notes; one 30-min single-task attention block. AI pre-check: what is the actual question / what's the goal / what would I say from scratch.
4. Social: call instead of text for important things, say yes and show up, say the scary thing, be the one who initiates ("chief of your village"), choose one deeper friendship this year over many light ties.

**2 immediate action points**
- Cast one identity vote within 12 hours: when an easier and a harder option appear, pre-commit to the harder one.
- Run one micro-experiment for 7 days from any category.
