# Craniofacial Claims Fact-Check (looksmaxxing wiki vs. peer-reviewed literature)

Audit of 12 facial-bone claims from a looksmaxxing wiki. 1 supported, 3 partly supported,
8 contradicted or unsupported. Every verdict below cites a real paper or an official body.
Searched 2026-10-14 via NCBI E-utilities (esearch/efetch), PMC full text, and AAO.

## Summary

Only one wiki claim survives intact in spirit (ramus height tracks bite force). The rest either
trace to a real paper the wiki mangled (bruxism 300x), invert the anatomy (bizygomatic:bigonial),
conflate two different quantities (bone turnover vs. bone shape), or have no evidence at all
(mewing in adults, gum chewing adding jaw width).

## Takeaways

Stop citing "300x" and "10% per year" as evidence for jaw training. Both are real numbers from
real papers that mean something else:
- 300x = odds ratio from a study whose control group was 12-18-year-olds, not adult non-bruxers.
- 10%/yr = bone mass replacement, net-zero, says nothing about shape.
- 1.35 bizygomatic:bigonial = inverted ratio definition + arithmetic error inside the wiki itself.

Gonial angle target of 90 deg is wrong by ~35 degrees (norm ~120-130 deg). Mewing has no adult
skeletal evidence; AAO states it explicitly. Facial masculinity-attractiveness is contested, not
established.
