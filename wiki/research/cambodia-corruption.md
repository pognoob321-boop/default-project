# Corruption in Cambodia

## Summary

Cambodia's corruption is structural, embedded in a patronage-based political system built since the post-Khmer Rouge era. The Cambodian People's Party (CPP) gained 100% legislative control in 2018 after dissolving the only viable opposition. The judiciary lacks independence, the Anti-Corruption Unit has no genuine autonomy, and the 2010 Anti-Corruption Law provides no whistleblower protection — whistleblowers can be jailed up to 6 months. Cambodia's Transparency International CPI score has declined every year: 24 (2022) → 22 (2023) → 21 (2024) → 20 (2025), ranking 163rd out of 182 countries — second-to-last in ASEAN, ahead only of Myanmar.

## Key Statistics

- 37% of public service users paid a bribe in the past 12 months (Transparency International Global Corruption Barometer, Asia 2020)
- World Justice Project ranked Cambodia 139th out of 140 countries for corruption (2022)
- U.S. State Department (2024) explicitly names "systemic corruption" as dissuading foreign investors
- The 2010 Anti-Corruption Law provides penalties up to 15 years but has been inadequately implemented
- The ACU received 520 complaints in 2024, including 141 anonymous submissions

## Most Corrupt Sectors

| Sector | Nature of Corruption |
|--------|---------------------|
| Law Enforcement | Perceived as most corrupt; police survive on bribes; buy positions |
| Judiciary | Courts protect the powerful; 73 mob justice killings 2000–2018 |
| Healthcare | Bribery required for medical services |
| Construction/Infrastructure | Extensive red tape; bribe demands for permits commonplace |
| Traffic/Transportation | Traffic police funded by bribes |
| Land | Land disputes rife with no reliable independent dispute resolution |
| Online Scam Operations | Major centre for transnational online scam operations |
| Casino/Gambling | Connected to transnational crime |

## Why It Persists

1. **Patronage system**: State legitimacy is built through elite loyalty rather than institutions
2. **Single-party rule**: CPP holds 100% of seats since 2018; no political competition
3. **Weak judiciary**: Courts lack independence and protect those in power
4. **No whistleblower protection**: Under the 2010 Anti-Corruption Law
5. **Suppressed civil society**: NGOs, unions, and opposition face regulation and intimidation

## Reform Efforts

- **Anti-Corruption Unit (ACU)**: Established 2010; lacks genuine independence
- **Public Procurement Law (2012)**: Passed to fight corruption in public procurement
- **National Arbitration Center (2010/2012)**: Alternative dispute resolution mechanism
- PM Hun Manet (since 2023) has adopted tough rhetoric but analysts conclude it is not translating into meaningful reform
