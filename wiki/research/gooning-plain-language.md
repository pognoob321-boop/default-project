# Gooning and Masturbating, Explained Simply

Plain-language version of [Gooning Evidence Review](gooning-evidence-review.md). No jargon, and no coy wording — gooning and masturbation are just the names of the behaviour, so we use them. If you read only one section, read "The One Thing That Actually Matters."

## The One Thing That Actually Matters

**Gooning itself is medically harmless.** Almost every scary thing you've heard about it is either wrong, or true of something else — usually porn addiction groups, not the practice.

The real risk comes from exactly four things:
1. **How long** the session runs
2. **What time** it happens
3. **Whether you orgasm** or not
4. **Whether you can stop** when you decide to

That's it. Number 4 is the only one that turns gooning into an actual medical or mental-health problem.

## What gooning is

**Masturbating** normally: you get aroused, you masturbate, you orgasm, you're done. Takes a few minutes.

**Gooning** is prolonged edging — staying in the "almost there" state on purpose for a long time, often hours, usually with porn. You're chasing the feeling of being on the edge, not the orgasm at the end. A person who goons is a *gooner*. The word showed up around 2005, spread from 4chan and Tumblr, and got popular again around 2023.

Roughly 1 in 4 adults have gooned at least once, based on a 2025 survey. Most gooners are men aged 18–25.

## What's actually GOOD

**1. Gooning doesn't physically hurt you.** The only effect doctors actually document is a dull ache in the testicles, called "blue balls." It's uncomfortable and it goes away on its own. Done.

**2. Orgasm helps you sleep.** A 2023 sleep-diary study found: partnered sex or masturbating **with an orgasm** → better sleep and faster sleep onset, in both men and women. Real measured effect.

**3. Regular ejaculation seems mildly protective for the prostate.** Two long-term studies — one tracked about 30,000 men for nearly 20 years — found men who ejaculated 20+ times a month were about **20% less likely** to develop prostate cancer than men who ejaculated 4–7 times a month.

Two honest caveats: it's observational, so it might not be causation. And separately, "masturbating a lot" on its own showed **no** benefit — it's the number of ejaculations that tracks, not how you got there.

**4. Edging is a legitimate technique for lasting longer in bed.** Start–stop and squeeze methods are established treatments for premature ejaculation. That's edging with a clear goal and an actual finish line — the exact opposite of gooning.

## What's actually BAD

**1. Not orgasming is the bad part.** This is the big one and almost nobody knows it.

- If you masturbate without orgasm, people in that sleep study reported **worse** sleep — longer to fall asleep, poorer quality. Men felt this most strongly.
- There's a reason. Your brain releases a hormone called **prolactin** when you orgasm — it's a chemical "shut down for now" signal. Get aroused without orgasming and prolactin never arrives.
- So gooning keeps the reward engine running and never pulls the brake. Whether that causes long-term damage has never actually been measured. But it's the defining feature of the practice, and it's the one version of the behaviour that measurably hurts sleep.

**2. Long sessions cost you sleep, which costs you hormones.** This is about the clock, not the activity.

- Cutting sleep to 5 hours a night for a week drops daytime testosterone by 10–15% (*JAMA*, 2011).
- About 70% of growth-hormone pulses happen during deep sleep in the first hours of the night.

A gooning session from 11pm to 2am isn't just "not helping." It eats the exact window where your body does hormone repair work.

**3. Rubbing too hard causes real but boring damage.** Soreness, chafed skin on the penis, and temporary loss of penile sensitivity if you always use the same tight grip. Genuinely reversible — normally back to normal within 3–4 weeks with less frequency and a looser grip. "Nerve damage" from masturbation is basically a myth; if sensation is genuinely gone, the usual culprits are anxiety and a tense pelvic floor, not destroyed nerves.

**4. Some gooning sessions go on for *days*.** Documented, rare, and occasionally serious enough to require hospital care. If a session is heading past a few hours, that's a sign something other than pleasure is driving it.

## Two Big Myths, With Evidence Against Them

### Myth 1: "Gooning permanently damages your dopamine receptors and you can never feel normal again."

The most-repeated claim in this whole space. **It has been tested directly with brain scans, and it failed.**

Researchers scanned men with compulsive porn use and men without, using a PET scan that measures dopamine D2/D3 receptors in the striatum — the brain's reward centre.

Result: **no difference between the two groups.** Not in receptor availability. Not in frontal brain blood flow. And within the compulsive group, how severe someone's porn use was had **zero** relationship to their receptor levels. The paper's own conclusion: they found "no support for sharing similar neurobiological alterations as previously reported in other addictive modalities."

For comparison, reduced D2/3 receptors **is** reliably found in cocaine and methamphetamine users. So this story was borrowed from drug research and applied to a behaviour nobody has ever scanned over time.

Same for "dopamine detox" — quitting all enjoyable things to "reset" your dopamine. It doesn't work that way.

### Myth 2: "Porn gave me porn-induced ED."

More complicated than Myth 1, because there *is* a real thing here — but the popular version is wrong.

**What doesn't hold up:**
- Reviewing 11 studies: watching porn isn't a meaningful risk factor for sexual problems. How *much* you watched didn't predict it either.
- A one-year longitudinal study: nothing about porn use predicted whether erectile problems appeared or worsened. No causal link.
- The one lab study using actual human partners found the *opposite* — men who watched more porn were **more** aroused by their real partner.
- Anxiety is the best-established predictor of ED, measured **inside** the NoFap community itself (669 people). Anxiety explained it. Porn frequency explained none of it.

**The documented harm runs the other direction.** The most-engaged members of those communities reported the *most* erectile problems, depression, anxiety, and suicidal thoughts. In a preregistered study of 587 men doing "reboots," **28.9% said their most recent failed streak made them feel suicidal.** The same researchers noted that 95% of men with porn concerns at the start of a study recovered on their own within 5 years, with no program at all.

**What does have support:** men who prefer masturbating with porn over partnered sex do report higher rates of erectile problems. And a small number of clinicians report some patients' arousal improved after they stopped internet porn. Real, but clinical observation rather than trial evidence — and the honest read is that for most of those men, the erection problem was **anxiety about the erection** rather than the porn.

Shame is the mechanism that turns it into a problem: you're told you broke your brain → you get scared → fear of failure causes the very failure you feared → self-fulfilling prophecy.

## How to know if it's actually a problem

**Not a disorder** if: you like it, you can stop, nothing in your life is suffering, and the only problem is that you feel guilty. Guilt is explicitly excluded from the medical definition. A high sex drive alone is not a disorder. Neither is normal teenage masturbation.

**Worth treating as a real problem** if you honestly tick all of these, over 6+ months:
1. You've tried to stop or cut down many times and failed
2. It's taken over — skipping sleep, school, work, hygiene, or responsibilities
3. You've kept going despite actual consequences
4. You keep doing it even when you get nothing out of it
5. Something important in your life is genuinely damaged

That's the official medical test, written by the WHO (ICD-11). All five true → real problem, get help. Only the guilt true → the guilt is the bigger problem.

## What actually helps, if it IS a problem

**Best first step: therapy.** Specifically CBT (cognitive behavioural therapy) — the guideline-recommended first treatment from the World Federation of Societies of Biological Psychiatry.

Evidence is small but real:
- An RCT of group CBT for hypersexuality cut both compulsive behaviour and depression
- An 8-week mindfulness-based relapse-prevention programme (pilot, 13 men) cut porn time plus anxiety and depressive symptoms
- An acceptance-and-commitment-therapy programme showed improvements still holding at 1 year (small, uncontrolled)
- **Medications:** SSRIs and naltrexone, used off-label. No drug is approved for this. Few trials, weak evidence, a doctor decides.

**What to ignore:** superpowers claims, permanent testosterone boosts, the 90-day reboot as a cure, "porn-induced ED" as a diagnosis, dopamine damage stories, day-by-day "flatline" timelines. The "flatline" — libido dropping during abstinence — is reported by about a quarter of people who try it, but NoFap's own FAQ admits: "we have yet to see any peer-reviewed academic research on it."

**Worst available option:** shame. It's now the single best-documented harm in this entire space.

## The 60-second version

Gooning isn't dangerous. Four things make it risky: how long, what time, whether you orgasm, and whether you can stop. Your brain does not get permanently damaged — the scan study says so plainly. Gooning isn't proven to cause lasting erectile problems, and the strongest predictor of those is anxiety. Orgasm helps you sleep; deliberately not having one hurts it. The real danger is a session that eats your deep sleep, or a habit you've lost control of — not anything happening inside your head.
