# Computational Irreducibility

The principle that certain computational processes cannot be "shortcut"—the only way to determine the outcome is to run the computation step by step. Formalized by Stephen Wolfram in *A New Kind of Science* (2002), it explains why many systems resist traditional mathematical analysis. If a system is computationally irreducible, no formula, algorithm, or observer (not even a hypothetical omniscient demon) can predict its final state faster than simulating it directly. Simple systems with repetitive or nested patterns are always reducible, but once behavior becomes complex or chaotic, irreducibility is the generic case. The concept connects to undecidability (questions about infinite-time behavior may be formally unanswerable) and has implications for physics, emergence, and the limits of scientific explanation.
