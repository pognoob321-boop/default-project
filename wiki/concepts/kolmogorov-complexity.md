# Kolmogorov Complexity

The length of the shortest computer program that produces a given output, measured in a fixed universal description language. Also known as algorithmic complexity, program-size complexity, or algorithmic entropy. Introduced independently by Andrey Kolmogorov (1965), Ray Solomonoff (1960–1964), and Gregory Chaitin (1966–1969). A string with low Kolmogorov complexity (relative to its length) has regularities that allow compression; a string with high Kolmogorov complexity is effectively incompressible and considered "random" in the algorithmic sense. The theory is independent of the choice of universal computer up to an additive constant, establishing fundamental limits on compression and connecting to computability theory, randomness, and information theory. Kolmogorov complexity is uncomputable—one cannot in general determine the shortest program for a given string—which links it to the halting problem and undecidability.
