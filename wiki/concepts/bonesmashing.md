# Bonesmashing

> Dangerous practice of hitting facial bones with blunt objects to trigger bone remodeling. Strongly discouraged by medical professionals.

## Definition

Bonesmashing is the practice of intentionally striking facial bones with blunt objects (hammers, mallets, etc.) in hopes of triggering bone remodeling and creating stronger/more defined facial structure.

## The Theory (Unscientific)

Proponents believe that controlled blunt force trauma to facial bones will trigger Wolff's Law (bone adapts to mechanical load), causing bones to become thicker, denser, or reshape over time.

## Why It's Dangerous

- **Permanent bone deformity**: Uneven bone growth or fracture
- **Nerve damage**: Facial nerve injury causing numbness or paralysis
- **Chronic pain**: TMJ dysfunction, jaw pain
- **Dental problems**: Tooth damage, bite misalignment
- **Infection risk**: Open wounds, hematoma
- **No controlled evidence**: Zero scientific support

## Medical Consensus

- **No respected doctor endorses bonesmashing**
- Published concerns in *J Stomatol Oral Maxillofac Surg* (2024): "Urgent concern regarding 'bone smashing', a dangerous trend on TikTok"
- GQ (2026): "As looksmaxxing enters our lexicon, the practice of bonesmashing — tapping your face with a hammer — is trailing close behind"
- **BBC (2026)**: Describes bonesmashing as an "extreme" practice within looksmaxxing

## Safe Alternatives for Jawline Definition

1. **Body fat reduction** (highest impact — reveals existing structure)
2. **Mewing** (low risk, limited evidence for adults)
3. **Chewing hard foods/mastic gum** (masseter development)
4. **Resistance training** (body composition change)
5. **Jawline filler** ($1K-$4K, semi-permanent)
6. **Jaw implants** ($5K-$40K, permanent)
