# Appealmaxxing

> Maximizing overall attractiveness appeal beyond just physical features — encompassing aura, presence, body language, charisma, and lifestyle signals.
>
> **Evidence base:** [[non-facial-attractiveness-evidence]]. Every claim below carries a verdict.
> Undated claims on this page were folklore until 2026-10-17.

## Definition

Appealmaxxing is a holistic approach to maximizing attractiveness that goes beyond bone structure
and physical features — the complete package of how someone comes across in real life.

## The finding that reorders the whole practice

Lidborg, Cross & Boothroyd (2022, eLife 11:e65031) meta-analysed 96 studies and 177,044 men on
six masculine traits against actual mating and reproductive outcomes:

| Trait | → mating (r) | → reproduction |
|---|---|---|
| **Body masculinity (strength/muscle)** | **.133** | **consistent** |
| Voice pitch (lower) | .132 | ns |
| Testosterone | .093 | ns |
| Facial masculinity | .080 (**not significant**) | — |
| Height | .057 | ns |

**Muscularity is the only trait that holds up for both mating and reproduction. Facial masculinity
is not statistically significant.** This is the single most useful thing to know on this page: the
best-supported physical lever is the gym, not the jaw.

## Components, with verdicts

### Body — SUPPORTED, the strongest lever
- **Adiposity into the healthy range, not shredded.** Barber (2025): inverted-U, optimum BMI ~23-27,
  measured on DXA images across China, Lithuania and the UK. Adiposity beat shoulder-to-waist ratio
  in all three countries — this **overturns** the classic "V-taper is law" literature, which could
  not separate the two variables.
- **Upper body carries the signal.** N = 1,742: every upper-body muscle cleared 50% on "does this
  muscle affect men's attractiveness?"; almost no lower-body muscle did.
- **Muscularity is itself inverted-U** — both very low and very high muscle are dispreferred.
- **For men, "attractive" composition ≈ "healthy" composition** (Brierley 2016). This is not true for
  women. Do not port female "get thinner" advice onto men.

### Facial Appeal — PARTLY, and weaker than assumed
- **Hair, grooming, skincare**: cheap, fast, visible. Supported mostly through the halo effect below.
- **Facial ratios / angularity / dimorphism scoring**: see [[looksmaxxing-attractiveness-claims-audit]].
  The ratio table is looksmax.org folklore, not research, and facial masculinity fails to predict
  mating outcomes at all.
- **Chin implants: null.** Parsa et al. 2019 — chin augmentation produced no significant change on
  any outcome, and the masculinity gain across male procedures was +0.13 [−0.03, 0.30], not significant.

### Voice — PARTLY SUPPORTED, and the controllable part is loudness, not pitch
- Lower pitch predicts attractiveness curvilinearly (inverted U) — very low pitch is *not* better.
- But F0 explains only **2%** of men's height, while vocal-tract-length estimates explain 10% — and
  the formant cues that track body size do **not** track attractiveness.
- **Actionable:** projection (loudness, χ² = 7.52, p = .006) and pitch variability (χ² = 34.00,
  p < .001). Both are behavioural, not anatomical.
- Averaging your voice out does nothing for attractiveness.

### Height — SUPPORTED but tiny; not a lever after growth
- r = .057. Real couples show it far more weakly than people claim to prefer (Stulp 2013: preference
  slopes were 4× the realised slopes).
- The threshold is **"average or above, not extremely tall"** (Barkow 2014, N = 165,606).
- Buy elevator shoes for social optics, never for mating returns.

### Posture and movement — SUPPORTED that it matters; male data is thin
- Dynamic cues are as important as static ones for attractiveness ratings.
- **Ordinary walking beats performed dance** (mean diff 0.47, p < .001).
- Evidence gap: gait-attractiveness research is almost entirely female. Male parameters are unstudied.

### Scent — CONTRADICTED
- The MHC/"chemical attraction" story is **null** across four meta-analyses, with documented
  publication bias (Havlíček et al. 2020). One study found real couples' odours were *more* similar
  than random pairs — the opposite of the claim — and fragrance eliminated the effect.
- Actionable: hygiene, light scent. Don't expect a compatibility effect.

### Teeth — SUPPORTED
- Tooth **colour** is the single largest smile lever (effect −0.56). Diastema is comparable (−0.55).
- Laypeople cannot detect even a 4 mm dental midline deviation. Sub-millimetre incisor proportion
  work is invisible to non-professionals — skip it.

### Personality and behaviour — SUPPORTED, and underrated
- Conscientiousness (r = .16) and agreeableness (r = .11) assortatively mate across 199 studies, and
  both independently predict relationship satisfaction (Barelds 2010, 3,848 participants).
- **People assort on values and education far more than on bodies** (political values r = .58;
  height r = .27).
- What people *think* you are beats what you are at first contact.

### Presence / confidence / charisma — PARTLY, mostly via the halo
- Attractive people are judged and treated more positively (11 meta-analyses); job outcomes d = .37.
- **The important limit:** the halo is competence-shaped. Largest for social competence, intermediate
  for potency, and **near zero for integrity and concern for others**. Looking better changes how
  competent you seem, not how kind you seem.
- Direction is usually negativity bias — being unattractive is the disadvantage.

### Lifestyle / status — SUPPORTED and asymmetric
- Income works for men and barely for women: +32% to +156% more first contacts (Hitsch et al. 2010),
  college degree +35%. Causally confirmed by a Norwegian admission-discontinuity study where
  marginal elite admission moved **women** into elite households and did nothing for men.
- **Men already try as hard as women** — 215 vs 238 min/day on appearance across 93 countries.
  The "men don't put in effort" premise is false.

## What has no evidence — do not build a protocol on these

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Peacocking | **No evidence** |
| Negging | **No evidence** |
| Cosmetic surgery as a reliable male lever | **Contradicted** — the only well-powered test is null (+0.08/10, P > .54) |
| Scent / MHC chemistry | **Contradicted** — meta-analytic null |
| "Structural powerlessness" | **Dead** — rejected by its own originator, Buss |
| Height as a controllable lever | **Not after growth** |
| Mate-choice copying as a face-specific mechanism | **Failed replication** |

## Appeal vs. Bone Structure

- Facial harmony matters more than any single feature; features working together beat feature perfection.
- **Static photos ≠ real life.** Dynamic cues carry as much weight as static ones.
- The strongest evidence-backed levers are the ones nobody posts about: muscle, conscientiousness, income.

## The real-life factor

A face in a still photo is not judged the same way as a face in motion and in person: expression,
voice, posture, eye contact, social confidence. This is now backed by data rather than intuition —
dynamic cues perform at least as well as static ones in attractiveness ratings.
