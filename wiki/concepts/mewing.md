# Mewing

> Practice of maintaining correct tongue posture for facial bone remodeling. Part of the orthotropics movement.
>
> ⚠️ **Verified 2026-10-14.** The AAO position below is *understated*. The
> American Association of Orthodontics does more than decline to recommend
> mewing — it names concrete harms and notes that **John Mew lost his
> orthodontic license over unsupported claims.**
>
> AAO press release, 2024-01-22, President Myron Guymon: *"There's no
> scientific evidence to support its claims of reshaping the jawline, and the
> potential risks outweigh any unproven benefits."* Named risks: **tooth
> loosening, bite misalignment, tooth wear, speech impairment**, and
> complications requiring prolonged orthodontic treatment.
>
> AAO consumer article, 2025-03-25: *"there's no current research that
> suggests the technique provides any benefit to your jawline or oral health."*
>
> **Note:** the widely-repeated claim that "tongue pressure is 5-10 g but
> 500-1000 g is needed for skeletal change" appears elsewhere in this wiki and
> is **fabricated**. Measured tongue pressure is ~52 kPa (Arakawa 2021,
> PMID 32799377); optimal tooth-movement force is 28-44 g tipping / 110-137 g
> translation (Li 2021, PMID 34183652). The "500-1000 g" figure appears to
> confuse gram-force with **bite force of 500-700 newtons**.
>
> What is genuinely supported: **orofacial myofunctional therapy** (a clinical
> discipline, not the meme) has real evidence for reducing obstructive sleep
> apnea severity — Saba et al. 2024, *Laryngoscope* 134:480-495
> (PMID 37606313). That is a different intervention from unsupervised tongue
> posting, and it belongs to a clinician.

## Definition

Mewing is the practice of maintaining correct tongue posture — keeping the entire tongue pressed against the roof of the mouth (palate), lips sealed, and breathing through the nose. Named after British orthodontists John and Michael Mew.

## The Theory

Correct tongue posture creates upward and forward pressure on the palate during development, supporting more forward facial growth (stronger, more defined jaw and midface). Poor posture allows downward facial growth (longer face, weaker jaw, narrower palate).

## Correct Technique

1. **Tongue Placement**: Entire tongue presses against roof of mouth; tip rests just behind front teeth without touching
2. **Suction Hold**: Light suction rather than forceful pushing; sustainable and low-effort
3. **Teeth Position**: Lightly touching or slightly apart; do not clench (causes TMJ tension)
4. **Lips**: Closed, no tension or force; should be invisible from outside
5. **Breathing**: Always through nose

## Scientific Evidence

### Supported
- **Bone remodeling is real**: Bones adapt to mechanical load over time (same mechanism behind orthodontics)
- **For children/teens**: Reasonable evidence that oral posture influences facial development
- **Chronic mouth breathing**: Consistently linked to longer face shape, narrower palate, weaker jaw development
- **Orofacial function**: 2025 systematic reviews found genuine evidence for improved nasal breathing, swallowing mechanics, tongue-thrust correction
- **Sleep apnea**: 2021 meta-analysis found myofunctional therapy significantly reduced apnea-hypopnea index

### NOT Supported
- **Adult bone change**: No credible evidence mewing reshapes jawline/facial structure past early childhood
- **Journal of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery (2019)**: Insufficient evidence for bone change from tongue posture alone in adults
- **British Dental Journal (2014)**: Called for prospective controlled trials
- **American Association of Orthodontists**: Does not recommend mewing for adults

## Honest Summary

- **For developing children/teens**: May support better facial development; highest potential
- **For adults**: Unlikely to visibly reshape bone; won't hurt but don't expect results
- **For orofacial function**: Genuine benefits for breathing, swallowing, tongue posture
- **NOT a replacement for orthodontic treatment** when needed
