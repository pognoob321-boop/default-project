# Research Methods

Structured approaches to deep research, synthesized from multiple sources.

## Core Frameworks

### Top-Down vs Bottom-Up
- **Top-down**: Start with hypothesis/goal → research to validate/narrow. Faster, risks confirmation bias.
- **Bottom-up**: Start with foraging (scanning papers, videos, articles) → patterns emerge → topic crystallizes. Slower but discovers unexpected connections.
- **Bridge**: Most effective = bottom-up to discover, top-down to execute.

### Depth × Breadth Matrix
| | Low | High |
|---|-----|------|
| **Depth** | Survey level | PhD/engineer depth |
| **Breadth** | General survey | Ultra-niche specialization |

Modern research leans **deep + narrow**. Cannot maximize both simultaneously.

### CRAP Test (4 Criteria)
From library science, taught in video as **CRAP** (not CRAAP):
- **Current** — Timeliness, validity, obsolescence
- **Relevance** — Contributes to *your* conversation? Same topic ≠ same angle
- **Authority** — Peer review, expert author, citation count, venue
- **Purpose** — Author goal, funding, context, bias

Accuracy (extra A in CRAAP) is a common variant but not in original video.

### 4-Level Funnel + Commonplace Book
1. **Inspection** — Glance filter (abstract, blurb, TOC)
2. **To Read** — Worth deeper dive
3. **Insights** — Extract relevant ideas, connect to your questions
4. **Order** — Synthesize into narrative/structure
5. **Commonplace Book** — Hub where ordered insights combine (Obsidian, Zotero, Notion). Not a funnel level — the destination.

### Critical Thinking Habits
- **Multiple sources** — 6+ for any claim; different POVs, cross-reference
- **Hesitate to judge** — Doubt yourself; need full picture before conclusions
- **Seek challenges** — Read disagreeable sources; confirmation bias = mental masturbation
- **Paragraph audit** — Break into: points, arguments, evidence, assumptions, limitations
- **Know your limits** — Papers written for experts; use shallow media as bridge (aware they're filters)

### Output as Goal
- No output = drift. Produce tangible artifact (article, video, essay, wiki) → accountability forces structure.
