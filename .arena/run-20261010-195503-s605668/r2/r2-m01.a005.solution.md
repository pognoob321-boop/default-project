## 1. Choose Your Topic: Two Approaches

### 1.1 Top-Down Approach
**Video definition**: Choose a title/focus first, then base research on it. Quicker, more intuitive. You know what you need.
- Start with a general idea -> narrow as you learn -> arrive at precise question.
- Risk: **confirmation bias** -- you only look for evidence supporting your initial idea.

### 1.2 Bottom-Up Approach
**Video definition**: Start with research, let the topic emerge. "Foraging" -- read what interests you, collect standout ideas/questions.
- Build knowledge base of interests -> questions/conversations/avenues appear naturally -> narrow down to linear pursuit.
- More time-consuming but better when you don't know what to focus on.
- **What modern researchers do**: notice observations -> new experiment/project.

### 1.3 Hybrid Tip (from both video + notes)
> Start bottom-up to find your topic, then switch top-down to fill gaps and add structure. (Video: "you can start with the bottom-up approach and then switch to the top-down")

> **User interpretation**: "You finish, you find that topic after the topic. You locked in, you researched about that topic. When you are done... scholars, they do this. They literally just find anything they want and research about it as hard as possible."

### 1.4 Curiosity as Compass (Video emphasis)
- Research needs **genuine interest**, not "should-know" lists.
- Forcing topics kills motivation (Ubisoft-game completionism analogy).
- Listen to what you think about, care about, want to talk about -- that pulls you naturally.

## 2. Depth & Breadth: The Trade-off Matrix

**Video metaphor**: Cross-section of a lake -- literal depth and breadth.

| Dimension | Range | What It Means |
|-----------|-------|---------------|
| **Depth** (left) | High school -> PhD+ | How far you take the research; ultra-specific, ultra-technical at deep end |
| **Breadth** (right) | Super general -> Ultra-niche | How wide your scope; "psychology" vs. "intersectional feminism in Warhammer 40K hive cities" |

**Key principles**:
- You generally **cannot have both** extremes simultaneously.
- **Shallow + wide**: General idea, gist, core concepts, no deep dive.
- **Deep + narrow**: Precise question, advances conversation in tiny corner of vast topic.
- **Modern research leans deep+narrow** -- precise questions advancing the field.
- **Hobbyists can compromise more** -- not constrained by publication requirements.
- **Not locked in**: Topic can change, narrow, branch over time.

> **User notes (partly garbled, repaired from transcript)**: "Death is depth... Breath is breadth... basically how good or how much effort you can put into your research... are you going to go super niche or super general?"

## 3. Gather Literature: Entry Points & Strong Sources

### 3.1 Shallow Media as Entry Points
**Video term**: "Shallow media" = anything digestible/easy to consume (videos, articles, blogs, popular editions, Wikipedia).
- **Purpose**: Build mental map, identify key terms, understand schools of thought, get case studies/examples.
- **No shame** in using these -- they're on-ramps, not destinations.
- **Example**: Philosophy noob encounters "moral anti-realism" in interview -> reads article/wiki/video -> gets map -> later ready for papers/books.

> **User note**: "When you are exploring new topic, you're not going straight forward... You're basically finding something easy... giving you a concept map... so you could actually understand it."

### 3.2 Deep Sources: Where to Look
- **Papers**: Google Scholar, JSTOR, ScienceDirect, ResearchGate, Semantic Scholar, **Consensus** (video creator's favorite).
- **Paywall workarounds**:
  - Add `filetype:pdf` to search
  - Check institutional access (university/library)
  - Anna's Archive, Sci-Hub (Sci-Hub is illegal in most jurisdictions; Anna's Archive is legally contested)
  - Email authors directly -- "regular people proud of their work"
- **Books**: Find "big dogs" -- authoritative, heavily cited, steering the conversation.
  - Inspect in libraries/bookshops: relevance, level, offerings.
  - **Don't read entire books** -- flick to relevant sections (abstract, conclusion, discussion for papers).

### 3.3 Source Quality Rules
- **Multiple sources mandatory** -- 2-3 books/articles != well-researched.
  - Cross-reference, build consensus, spot disagreements.
  - "Nice chunk of balanced strong sources representative of whole conversation."
- **Translations are filters** -- tone, liberties, personal views injected. Compare translations if possible (Odyssey example).
- **Reference manager obligatory** -- Zotero, Research Rabbit, Consensus. Never lose sources; link everywhere; back up.

> **User tips**: "Make sure it's relevant... you cannot just put all your energy into one place... find key words, phrases, topics relevant... you need more than [a few] sources... six... ten books and ten articles... good and strong sources... different POVs so you can connect the dots... books with translation could make big difference... do not lose your reference."

## 4. Schedule: Systems Over Willpower

### 4.1 Habitual Pattern (Atomic Habits principle)
> "You do not rise to the level of your goals, but fall to the level of your systems." -- James Clear
- Good study = repeated sequence keeping you consistent over months/years.
- Know exactly what, when, how long.
- **Never a diversion from life -- a part of it.**
- **Creator's system**: Every morning, 1-2 hours non-negotiable (read, write, start new, revisit old).

### 4.2 Associated Time & Space
- Mentally tune yourself: dedicated desk, cafe, library, "adventure" -- whatever works.
- "Invade a college library, even if not enrolled. No one's checking." (Allegedly in Minecraft.)

### 4.3 Journaling / Process Documentation
- Not a vlog -- personal notes: How's it going? What did you learn? Questions? Problems?
- **Purpose**: When life "trolls you" (demotivates, creates gaps), journal puts you back on track.
- Even one sentence works.

> **User interpretation**: "When life trolls you... you get demotivated... journal, your emotion is put out... you feel heard... this is my own interpretation."

## 5. Workflow: The 4-Level Funnel

**Purpose**: Transform scattered ideas from many sources into linear order / synthesis.

| Level | Name | Function |
|-------|------|----------|
| 1 | **Inspection** | Anything potentially relevant at a glance (blurb, abstract) |
| 2 | **To Read** | Sources worth deeper look (partial or whole) |
| 3 | **Insights** | Select ideas most relevant/interesting to your aims |
| 4 | **Order** | The story notes tell together -- connections, synthesis |

**Bottom of the funnel (tool, not a level)**: Your **commonplace book** -- Obsidian, notes app, mind maps -- where ideas mingle, form connections, reveal problems/disagreements.

**Video principle**: "Most new knowledge is not new at all. It's a synthesis of old ideas... unique perspective, fresh injection."

### 5.1 What to Do With Sources (Actionable Steps)
1. **Clip & rephrase** relevant insights/key points in your own words -- proves comprehension.
2. **Identify problems** -- what researchers struggle with, disagree on, are limited by -- reveals where your topic might go.
3. **Connect concepts** -- relate things even without obvious author-made links -- novel takes from unexpected connections.
4. **Create an index** (optional but recommended) -- self-written contents page, structure notes, create map. Canvas feature or any overview tool works.

> **User tips**: "Rephrase the text or phrases... highlight words that is important... find what's wrong, what questions they're missing, what ideas or weaknesses they have... connecting the dots... make an index is basically an overview or like a Wikipedia page."

## 6. Critical Thinking: The CRAAP Test & Beyond

### 6.1 The CRAAP Test (Video: "stole this pretty simple framework from the internet")

| Letter | Criterion | Questions |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| **C** | **Currency** | Recent? Still valid? Obsolete? (Matters more in fast-moving fields) |
| **R** | **Relevance** | Actually contributes to your conversation? Answers your question? Same topic != same problems/angle. |
| **A** | **Authority** | Respected journal? Real peer review? Author expert? History in field? Cited by serious people? Social proof? |
| **A** | **Accuracy** | Logical arguments? Enough evidence? Self-aware? Limitations acknowledged (good sign)? |
| **P** | **Purpose** | Author's goal? Why this research? Who funds it? Context? |

> **Note**: The standard acronym is **CRAAP** (Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose). The video calls it the "CRAP test" and merges the two A's (Authority + Accuracy) into one letter.

> **User misinterpretation (garbled)**: "Revolence... sources have different ideas, different points of views... you need to find something that goes with your view, something that aligns with your view, not something that's out of your view entirely." -- This misreads *Relevance* as "agreement with your view," which is the **confirmation bias** the video warns against (transcript line 263). The video defines Relevance as: does it answer your question? Same topic != same problems/angle.

### 6.2 Additional Critical Thinking Practices

1. **Read multiple sources** -- Single book = one person, not impartial, errors/biases/limits. Multiple sources audit each other -> clearer, balanced picture.

2. **Hesitate to make judgments** -- Don't rush to believe. Doubt yourself. "I almost feel like I'm an enemy to myself." Want to be proven wrong. Arrive slowly at conclusions.
   > **Video**: "Do not believe you are open-minded because you are not. First step to critical thinking is accepting that you're not open-minded."

   > **User expansion**: "You need to find 20, 10, I don't know how much, but a lot [of sources] to make your judgment good."

3. **Seek challenges** -- Read what you disagree with. Confirmation bias = "jerking yourself off." Changing mind = strength, not defeat.

4. **Break down paragraphs logically** -- Split into points, arguments, qualifiers. Check:
   - Do points/evidence back the claim?
   - Is evidence satisfying?
   - Assumptions made? Accepted? Limitations pointed out?

   > **User addition**: "He argues with them, like... checks, verifies if it's valid or not."

5. **Understand your limits** -- Technical papers written for researchers ("in the club"), not laymen ("NPC plebs"). Brute-forcing jargon = waste. Use shallow media/commentaries as bridges -- but remember **they're filters too** (omissions, framing choices).

   > **User interpretation**: "Understanding your limit is basically knowing what you could not and could not do... shallow media could be used to understand... but there could be a filter... this is my own interpretation."

## 7. Output: The Accountability Engine

**Video thesis**: Study feels meaningless without a driving purpose. Intrinsic motivation fails when it gets tough. Output gives you a goal to keep going.

### 7.1 Why Output Matters
- **Future-oriented**: "You're doing it for the future, not the past/present."
- **Accountability**: Forces scattered/jumbled ideas -> structure, flow, order for *someone else* to read.
- **Compound effect**: Essays, blogs, videos, Substack -- even typing to void builds discipline. Every successful creator started this way.

### 7.2 Suggested Outputs
- Essays, blog posts, videos, Substack newsletter.
- Not everything public or polished -- principle matters more than publication.
- "Convert your passion and effort into something people want to see."

> **User interpretation**: "Outputs he simply says is basically about giving you a goal to keep going... for Output you're just writing this article, researching about this for a future goal... you need to have a goal to set up to do a video about and that's it... this is my own interpretation."

## 8. Quick-Reference Checklist

| Phase | Action |
|-------|--------|
| **Topic** | Follow curiosity -> choose top-down (known goal) or bottom-up (foraging) or hybrid |
| **Scope** | Pick depth/breadth compromise; remember it's adjustable |
| **Literature** | Start with shallow media -> graduate to papers/books -> use multiple sources -> reference manager |
| **Schedule** | Build non-negotiable habit + dedicated space + journal + planning tool |
| **Workflow** | Funnel: Inspection -> To Read -> Insights -> Order (in your commonplace book) |
| **Process sources** | Clip & rephrase -> find problems -> connect concepts -> index |
| **Think critically** | CRAAP test + multiple sources + hesitate judgment + seek challenges + paragraph breakdown + know limits |
| **Output** | Produce something (essay, video, blog) -- even for audience of zero |

## 9. What the Video Gets Wrong / Weak Points

| Claim | Assessment |
|-------|------------|
| "Deep research is easy if you have a good system" | Overstatement -- systems help, but deep research remains cognitively demanding. |
| "Modern research leans deep+narrow" | True for academia; hobbyists/generalists may need different balance. |
| CRAAP test as complete framework | Useful heuristic, but doesn't replace domain expertise for evaluating technical accuracy. |
| "Peasant masses are literate now" / internet as curse | Rhetorical flourish; democratized access has massive benefits alongside noise. |
